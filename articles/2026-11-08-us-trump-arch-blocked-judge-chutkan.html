Judge blocks Trump's 250-foot arch for 14 days, hours after panel approval

A federal judge blocked work on President Donald Trump's 250-foot triumphal arch for 14 days, acting less than an hour after a planning panel gave the project its final approval following hours of public testimony against it.

8 November 2026 | US | By WPS Staff

Sources: CNN, USA Today

A federal judge blocked construction of President Donald Trump's proposed 250-foot triumphal arch in Washington for 14 days, acting within an hour of a planning panel giving the project its final approval late on Thursday, November 5. US District Judge Tanya Chutkan issued a temporary restraining order sought by the District of Columbia and Virginia.

The National Capital Planning Commission voted 8-4 to approve the project after more than eight hours of public testimony from more than 120 citizens, many of them veterans, who lined up to ask the panel to block it. The Trump administration had said it might begin construction immediately after the final vote, and the Justice Department had told the court that excavation and foundation work could begin as early as that week.

The judge's order bars the government from starting any construction-related activity at Memorial Circle, the traffic circle near the entrance to Arlington National Cemetery, across the Potomac River from the Lincoln Memorial. It covers construction, excavation, demolition, land disturbance, utility installation and road closures. Chutkan ordered federal officials to tell all employees and contractors about the order and to update the court by November 9 on their compliance.

The District of Columbia and Virginia sued the National Park Service and the Department of the Interior on October 31, calling the project legally indefensible and arguing that Congress never authorized the monument. The Trump administration contends that Congress approved the arch more than a century ago, when it authorized a bridge across the Potomac River. Opponents also say the arch would disrupt the sightline of landmarks including the Lincoln Memorial and Arlington House, and break the solemnity of funerals at Arlington National Cemetery.

Trump has promoted the arch, modeled after the Arc de Triomphe in Paris, as both a landmark and a military asset, saying it could house drones and snipers. He has said it will mark the nation's 250th anniversary and celebrate the American spirit and the American military. The Park Service has acknowledged the arch would have adverse effects on as many as 42 historic sites, with the most serious near the structure. The White House on Friday promoted a new website for the arch, run by a privately funded foundation.
