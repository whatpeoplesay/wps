Justice Alito will not retire immediately after Democratic win

Justice Samuel Alito, 76, does not plan to retire immediately despite Democrats winning control of the Senate, telling people close to him he will stay through this term.

5 November 2026 | Politics | By WPS Staff

Sources: CNN, USA Today, New York Times

Supreme Court Justice Samuel Alito, 76, does not plan to retire immediately, even though Democrats won control of the Senate in Tuesday's midterm elections, according to people close to him. He had already said last month that he planned to stay through the current court term, and he had ruled out stepping down during the lame-duck session of Congress.

Read More

The result has raised questions about the future of the court, because Alito and Justice Clarence Thomas, 78, are its oldest members and part of its 6-3 conservative majority. Democrats will take control of the Senate on 3 January 2027, while Republicans will keep control until then, so any retirement before that date would give President Donald Trump a short window to nominate a younger conservative successor.

Sources close to both justices told CNN on Wednesday that they did not expect either man to leave the court because of the election result. Both men took their seats on Wednesday to hear a case about a Texas death row prisoner, in a sign they were carrying on with the court's work.

Some Republicans have worried openly that the chance to strengthen the conservative majority may have passed. Florida Governor Ron DeSantis said no Supreme Court justice would be confirmed under Trump unless something happened in the next six weeks, and legal commentators said a lame-duck confirmation would face a high risk of failure, because several departing Republican senators were unlikely to support a nominee.

Alito has served on the court since 2006 and will be 78 when the next presidential election is decided in 2028. If a Democrat wins that election, he would have to wait at least four more years for a Republican president and a Republican Senate to choose his successor, a wait that has left some conservatives uneasy about the years ahead.
