UK watchdog proposes tougher scrutiny of Microsoft business software

Britain's competition watchdog proposed strategic market status for Microsoft in business software. The move could lead to new rules on how Microsoft bundles products such as Excel, Teams and Copilot.

16 October 2026 | Technology | By WPS Staff

Sources: Reuters, CNA, Computer Weekly

Britain's competition watchdog has proposed giving Microsoft a label called strategic market status in business software. The label could let the regulator step in over how Microsoft bundles products such as Excel, Teams and its Copilot AI assistant.

Read More

The Competition and Markets Authority started its investigation in May. It looked at whether Microsoft's bundling of business software was unfair to rivals. The software reaches about 20 million monthly users in the UK.

The regulator found that Microsoft holds a strong position in PC operating systems, productivity software, security software, server systems and database software. It said Microsoft's strength comes from the barriers customers face when they try to switch to another provider.

Microsoft said it did not agree with the proposed decision. A company spokesperson said all its products, including Copilot, are open and can work with competing services. The regulator is still looking at whether customers can freely choose AI tools, whether rivals can compete with Copilot, whether security bundling limits choice, and how licensing affects cloud options.

The regulator will take views on the proposal and make a final decision by February 2027. If it goes ahead, Microsoft would be the third company under Britain's new digital market powers, after Google in search and mobile platforms and Apple in mobile platforms in October 2025. In June this year, the regulator set new conduct rules for Google's search services.
