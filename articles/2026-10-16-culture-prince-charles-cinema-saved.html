London Prince Charles Cinema saved with a 25-year lease

The Prince Charles Cinema in London has signed a 25-year lease with no break clause, securing its future after two years of uncertainty.

16 October 2026 | Culture | By WPS Staff

Sources: Screen Daily, NME

London Prince Charles Cinema has signed a 25-year lease for its building, securing the future of the venue after two years of uncertainty.

Read More

The lease has no break clause, giving the venue extra security over the coming two and a half decades. The venue shared the news in a social media post, thanking the 169,000 people who signed a petition launched early last year to save the cinema. The petition protested against the break clause in its landlord's latest contract, which would have allowed the cinema to be closed with only six months' notice.

The new statement also thanked the Soho Society, a community association for the central London district, for recommending that Westminster Council designate the cinema as an asset of community value, and the council for doing so. The media, the film community, and the venue's staff also received thanks from the cinema.

The Prince Charles Cinema building is owned by Zedwell LSQ Ltd, which itself is owned by Asif Aziz's Criterion Capital. Following the announcement, Criterion Capital chief executive Vaq Farooq said the company was delighted to have agreed terms which secure the cinema's future at Leicester Place. He said a 25-year commitment provides real security for the cinema and its fans, and that the company is investing in a new Zedwell Capsule hotel on the upper floors.

The Prince Charles lease comes three months after licensing restrictions were lifted on the Picturehouse Central members' bar, following a dispute with Zedwell, which also owns the Piccadilly building in which it is housed. The cinema had to restrict its members' bar, a popular meeting point for the UK film industry, to same-day ticket holders only. The restrictions were lifted at the start of July.
