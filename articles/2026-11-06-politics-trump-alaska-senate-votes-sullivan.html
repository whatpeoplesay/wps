Trump says Alaska Senate votes should go to Republican

President Donald Trump has said votes cast for two other candidates in Alaska's Senate race should be given to Republican Dan Sullivan, who is trailing Democrat Mary Peltola as counting continues.

6 November 2026 | Politics | By WPS Staff

Sources: Economic Times, Anadolu Agency

President Donald Trump has said the race for the United States Senate in Alaska is unfair and has demanded that votes cast for two other candidates be given to the Republican incumbent, Dan Sullivan. In a post on Truth Social on Thursday, Trump wrote that the votes "should be immediately given to the REAL Dan Sullivan, and the Election is over."

Read More

Sullivan is trailing the Democratic candidate, Mary Peltola, as votes are still being counted. With about three quarters of the vote counted, Peltola had about 48.2 percent of the vote, against about 47.9 percent for Sullivan, a gap of only about 640 votes. The two other candidates are Daniel J. Sullivan, who is running without a party and has almost the same name as the senator, and Gerald Heikes, a Republican. Daniel J. Sullivan had about 1.7 percent of the vote and Heikes had about 2.3 percent.

Trump called Daniel J. Sullivan the "fake" Sullivan and described Heikes as a "slug" put on the ballot by Democrats. Republicans have said the similarly named candidate was meant to confuse voters and take votes from the senator. Peltola has said her campaign played no part in bringing the other Sullivan into the race. Democrats have also said that candidates with similar names have appeared on ballots before, and that changing the rules for this race would not be fair.

Alaska uses ranked choice voting. If no candidate wins more than half of the first choice votes, the candidates with the fewest votes are removed one by one and their voters' next choices are counted instead. That count is planned for 18 November, and the result is due to be certified on 25 November. Trump is asking for something different: he wants all of the first choice votes for the two other candidates to go to the senator at once, whatever voters put as their second choice. Tens of thousands of early and absentee ballots were still uncounted, and the final result may not be known until mid-November.

The demand came two days after the midterm elections on Tuesday, in which Democrats won control of both the Senate and the House of Representatives. Trump first accepted the results on election night, but he has since complained about the count in Alaska. Florida Governor Ron DeSantis, a Republican, described the party's losses as close to an "extinction-level event" in many parts of the country.
