Istanbul hit by heaviest rainfall in 83 years as floods kill three

A heavy downpour shut down Istanbul, flooded homes and roads, and killed at least three people. Rescuers evacuated more than 3,000 people stranded by the floods.

16 October 2026 | World | By WPS Staff

Sources: Associated Press via Bastille Post, The National

A heavy downpour shut down Istanbul, flooded homes and roads, and killed at least three people, officials said on Thursday. Rescuers evacuated more than 3,000 people who were stranded by the floods in Turkey's largest city.

Read More

The rain fell hard on Wednesday night, flooding homes and businesses and filling roads and underpasses with water. In some areas, floodwater swept cars along streets and sent them crashing into each other. Traffic across the city came to a standstill.

It was the heaviest rainfall in the city in 83 years, said Murat Kurum, the minister for the environment, urbanisation and climate change. The Istanbul governor's office reported three deaths and said five other people were reported missing. A 14 year old was electrocuted after stepping into a puddle at a park in the Fatih district, and a 34 year old slipped on a sidewalk in the Sariyer district and hit their head, according to local media reports. The body of a third victim was found near another park in the Kagithane district.

The governor's office extended a school closure order to include Friday, and some state employees were placed on leave on Thursday. Residents were asked not to leave their homes unless needed, and motorcycles were barred from traffic until further notice.

In Sakarya province, about 150 kilometres east of Istanbul, a two storey house built on a riverbed collapsed in the floods, according to HaberTurk television. One person was reported missing in the neighbouring Kocaeli province. The country's weather agency predicted more showers for Istanbul and the surrounding area in the coming days. Kurum, who will chair the COP31 climate conference in Antalya next month, linked the floods to the climate crisis and said rainfall patterns were changing.
