US considering using mother of all bombs against Iran, sources say

US military strike plans include the possible use of the MOAB against Iranian energy and nuclear sites, if President Trump orders a return to combat operations.

16 October 2026 | US | By WPS Staff

Source: CNN

The US military has developed new strike plans that include possibly using the so-called mother of all bombs to target Iranian energy infrastructure and nuclear sites, if President Donald Trump orders a return to combat operations before or after the midterm elections, according to multiple US officials familiar with the matter.

The weapon is the GBU-43/B Massive Ordnance Air Blast Bomb, or MOAB, the largest non-nuclear bomb in the US arsenal. Using it for the first time in the conflict with Iran would mark a notable escalation, though there are doubts about how effective it would be. Trump said in an interview on Thursday on Newsmax that it is not nuclear at all, that it is just an extremely large bomb, and that he would not use nuclear weapons.

The plans include strikes against energy infrastructure and nuclear sites, including Pickaxe Mountain, where the US and Israel believe Tehran has stored parts of its nuclear programme. Trump has repeatedly threatened to strike Pickaxe and has said his goal is to ensure Iran never obtains a nuclear weapon.

Sources questioned whether the MOAB would work against deep underground sites. The bomb explodes before hitting the ground and can destroy an area equal to nine city blocks, but it likely cannot penetrate deep enough to destroy suspected nuclear sites at Pickaxe, which are believed to be protected by layers of granite. Its large blast radius would also raise concerns about civilian casualties if it is used near populated areas.

Trump could still use the weapon as a psychological tool. Officials said it is unlikely to force Iran to give in or to win a strategic military victory. A White House official told CNN it does not comment on sensitive military plans that may or may not exist, and added that Trump keeps all options on the table to ensure Iran can never have a nuclear weapon. A Pentagon official said the department plans for a wide range of operational scenarios.
