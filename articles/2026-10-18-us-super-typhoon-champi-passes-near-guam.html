Super Typhoon Champi passes just south of Guam as a Category 4 storm

Super Typhoon Champi passed about 10 miles south of Guam early Sunday as a Category 4 super typhoon. Most of the island felt weaker winds than feared, and early reports showed no widespread damage.

18 October 2026 | US | By WPS Staff

Sources: The Guam Daily Post, The Weather Network, Pacific Daily News

Super Typhoon Champi passed just south of Guam early on Sunday morning, close enough that weather officials said the island came near a devastating hit. The eye passed about 10 miles south of the southern tip of Guam at about 4:26 a.m. local time, just as the storm was upgraded to a super typhoon.

The upgrade came with sustained winds of 150 miles per hour, the super typhoon mark and the high end of Category 4. Category 5 begins at 157 miles per hour. On Saturday evening the storm had sustained winds of 130 miles per hour. Its eye was about five miles wide, and winds fell off fast within 10 to 15 miles of the center. The storm moved at 15 to 16 miles per hour, which cut the time Guam spent under its wind and rain.

Most of Guam felt tropical storm winds with typhoon force gusts instead of the full force of the storm. The weather service had warned that winds of 110 to 130 miles per hour with gusts to 160 could reach Guam by sunrise, along with 8 to 14 inches of rain. Gusts at the airport reached 80 to 85 miles per hour. By midday the island had seen 1 to 3 inches of rain, and wind gauges at the airport, Apra Harbor and Pago Bay did not record the sustained 74 miles per hour needed to declare typhoon conditions.

Early damage reports showed no widespread significant damage. The far south, nearest the center, had not been assessed, and the weather service planned to survey far southern Guam on Monday for wind damage and coastal flooding. The Joint Information Center said Sunday morning that the strongest typhoon force winds were centered over southern Guam. Officials told residents not to go outside to check damage, even during calm spells, because showers could bring strong gusts without warning.

The storm moved away as a Category 4 super typhoon. At 11 a.m. its center was about 95 miles west southwest of Guam, with sustained winds of 150 miles per hour, moving west northwest at 16 miles per hour. The tropical storm warning and typhoon watch for Tinian and Saipan were lifted at 11 a.m. The governor declared the all clear, Condition of Readiness 4, at 6 p.m. on Sunday. Forecasters said Champi could reach Category 5 by late Monday morning, and the Joint Typhoon Warning Center expected it to stay at super typhoon strength through Wednesday as it crossed the Philippine Sea.
