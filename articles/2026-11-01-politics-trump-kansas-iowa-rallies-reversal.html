Trump reverses course and heads to Kansas and Iowa rallies

President Donald Trump has reversed his decision to cancel his weekend rallies in Kansas and Iowa and will travel to both states on Sunday, two days before the November 3 midterm elections.

1 November 2026 | Politics | By WPS Staff

Sources: USA Today, Daily Express (AFP)

Donald Trump has changed his mind and will travel to Kansas and Iowa on Sunday, after first cancelling the two weekend rallies and switching them to phone events. The two stops, planned as a double-header, come two days before the November 3 midterm elections.

Read More

The Republican National Committee had announced that the Kansas rally set for Saturday, October 31, and the Iowa rally set for Sunday, November 1, would be cancelled. Trump said on Friday, October 30, that Republicans in both states called him and asked him to come in person, saying they wanted to make sure they win, and he quoted them asking for rallies of about 50,000 people each.

At his October 30 rally in Daytona, Florida, Trump said he had expected that event to be the last of his midterm blitz, adding that 28 days in a row of campaigning is a lot, and that his voice was operating at 49.2 percent. He said the rallies were being held to stop Democrats taking over Congress, and he repeated his promise to give every adult American citizen 5,000 dollars if Republicans keep control of both chambers, a promise experts have questioned on legal authority.

The reversal came a day after the cancellation, a move which had raised questions about his health and about the state of the race. Trump said on Friday that Republicans have a shot at doing well, while acknowledging that the party of a sitting president often suffers in midterm elections.

Republicans are defending a 53 to 47 majority in the Senate and a 218 to 214 majority in the House, and experts project Democrats will win at least the House, with Senate control hinging on a short list of races including Kansas and Iowa. Trump has put himself at the centre of the Republican midterm strategy, hoping to fire up his base, even as his approval ratings and public anger over the cost of living and the war in Iran may drag on Republican chances.
