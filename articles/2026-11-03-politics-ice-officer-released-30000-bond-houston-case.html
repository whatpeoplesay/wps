ICE Officer Released on $30,000 Bond After Houston Vehicle Case

An ICE officer indicted over a Houston vehicle incident was released on a $30,000 bond on Monday, as prosecutors and his lawyer disputed what happened.

3 November 2026 | Politics | By WPS Staff

Sources: CNN, TNND

An Immigration and Customs Enforcement officer accused of hitting a man with a vehicle during an enforcement operation in Houston was released on a $30,000 bond on Monday after appearing in court for a bond hearing.

Read More

A grand jury indicted Mario Alberto Morales on Friday on a charge of aggravated assault with a deadly weapon. Prosecutors say he struck Miguel Parra Hernandez with his vehicle on October 10, causing multiple leg fractures. His next court hearing is set for December 2.

Morales was driving an ICE vehicle near Houston when he began to pursue a vehicle driven by Parra Hernandez, according to an ICE spokesperson. Harris County District Attorney Sean Teare said Parra Hernandez had lived in Harris County for more than 30 years and had no criminal record and no deportation order at the time. Teare also said the other man in the vehicle was not the person ICE said it was trying to arrest.

The district attorney said video evidence contradicted the account that Parra Hernandez ran in front of the vehicle, and said the evidence in the case was clear. Morales's lawyer, David Lindenmuth, said the incident was an accident and that his client did not intend to strike Parra Hernandez. ICE called the claim that Morales struck the man intentionally reckless and false, and described the indictment as a political stunt.

At Monday's hearing, prosecutors asked for bond to be set at $75,000, while the judge set it at $30,000. Parra Hernandez suffered fractures and nerve damage, had surgery, and continues to face mobility limits and pain, according to his lawyer, who said he wants accountability and assurance that it will not happen to anyone else.
