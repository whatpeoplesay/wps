Unapproved peptides land 1,180 people in NSW emergency departments

NSW Health says 1,180 people visited emergency departments after using unapproved peptide products promoted by wellness influencers, with monthly cases rising from five to more than 217.

19 October 2026 | Health | By WPS Staff

Sources: 1News, NSW Government

More than 1,180 people have visited emergency departments in New South Wales after using unapproved peptide products, in a trend health officials link to wellness influencers on social media. NSW Health published the figures on Monday, covering 1 July 2025 to 30 September 2026.

Read More

Monthly presentations rose from five in July 2025 to more than 217 in September 2026. About a quarter of the patients were aged 16 to 24. People aged 25 to 34 made up 32 percent, and people aged 35 to 44 made up 26 percent. Men made up 51 percent of the patients.

The products are marketed online as aids for weight loss, muscle growth, injury repair, and reversing ageing. Retatrutide, an experimental weight loss drug, was named in 71 percent of the presentations. Other named products included BPC-157, GHK-Cu, TB-500, and CJC-1295. NSW Health says none of these are approved in Australia for any purpose. Some peptide medicines are approved by the Therapeutic Goods Administration, but many products advertised online are not.

Patients arrived with abdominal pain, nausea and vomiting, chest pain, anaphylaxis, and other allergic reactions. Health Minister Ryan Park said the products carry real health risks and add pressure to public hospitals. He said professional looking packaging does not guarantee a product is safe.

Chief Health Officer Dr Kerry Chant said a professional looking website, label, purity claim, reviews, or influencer endorsement is not proof of what is inside a product. NSW Health urges people not to buy or use unapproved products, especially those promoted through social media, online retailers, overseas suppliers, or informal sellers, and to talk to their usual doctor about risks, benefits, and safer options.
