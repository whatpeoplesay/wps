Chinese brands drop Mbappe after video sparks backlash in China

Two major Chinese brands have ended their deals with Kylian Mbappe after a video spread online in which he appeared to joke about the country's former leader, sparking a debate among football fans.

27 October 2026 | Sports | By WPS Staff

Sources: Al Jazeera, Associated Press

Two big brands in China have ended their deals with French football star Kylian Mbappe after a video spread online in which he appeared to joke about the country's former leader. Drinks maker Dongpeng and dairy giant Mengniu both said on Tuesday that they were stopping all work with the Real Madrid forward with immediate effect.

Read More

The video shows Mbappe talking about the funniest meme of himself he had seen, a picture with his face placed on the body of what he called a Chinese dictator. The picture appears to point to a well-known meme that puts his face on an image of Mao Zedong, the founding leader of modern China. News agencies reporting the story noted that the video itself could not be verified right away, and Mbappe's representatives could not be reached for comment.

Both companies used the same kind of wording in their statements. Dongpeng said it firmly opposes any behaviour that offends national sentiments, while Mengniu said it firmly opposes any words or actions that hurt the feelings of the Chinese people. A third company, cheese maker Milkground, also announced it was ending its agreement, according to AP. In China, jokes about Mao are seen as disrespectful and remain a sensitive subject.

The news spread fast on Chinese social media, where the end of the Dongpeng deal became the top-trending topic on Weibo. Mbappe had been hugely popular in China, with millions of followers there, and adverts showing him drinking a Dongpeng sports drink were shown widely during this year's World Cup. China's foreign ministry also commented, saying words and deeds that hurt the feelings of the Chinese people will naturally meet opposition from all parts of society.

The row came one day after Mbappe lost out to Harry Kane for the Ballon d'Or award at a ceremony in London on Monday. Social media platform RedNote, which had a content partnership with Mbappe that ended in December 2024, said it would remove related promotional material and also spoke against conduct that hurts national sentiment.
