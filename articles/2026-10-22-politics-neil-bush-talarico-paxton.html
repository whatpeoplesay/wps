Two Bushes back Democrat in Texas Senate race

Neil Bush voted early for Democrat James Talarico over Republican Ken Paxton in the Texas Senate race, and Barbara Bush donated to a PAC backing Talarico.

22 October 2026 | Politics | By WPS Staff

Sources: USA Today, Al Jazeera

Neil Bush, the brother of former President George W. Bush, announced on Tuesday that he voted early for Democrat James Talarico over Republican Ken Paxton in the Texas Senate race. He shared the news in a long Facebook post, saying it was his first-ever political post. He wrote that he had stayed quiet about politics online because he did not want to offend friends with different views, but that he was breaking that habit because of this vote.

Bush said his choice came from deep concern over Paxton’s record and genuine admiration for Talarico’s character and style of politics. He wrote that he has been troubled by Paxton’s conduct as Texas attorney general, including his push to challenge the 2020 presidential election results in other states. He also said President Donald Trump has done enormous damage to the Republican Party, and that character matters more than party affiliation.

Barbara Bush, the former president’s daughter, has also put her support behind Talarico. She donated to a political action committee backing his campaign, a move that drew strong reactions online over the weekend. The two announcements turned the race into one of the most talked-about contests in the country. A Marist poll released on October 14 showed Talarico at 47 percent and Paxton at 45 percent. Democrats have not won a Senate seat in Texas since 1988.

The endorsements are the latest sign of division among Texas Republicans. Paxton, the Texas attorney general, defeated incumbent Senator John Cornyn in the Republican primary run-off on May 26, after Trump endorsed him and called him a “MAGA Warrior.” Some Republicans have questioned that move, pointing to Paxton’s vulnerabilities. He was impeached by the Republican-controlled Texas House, though the Texas Senate later acquitted him.

Thirty-five Senate seats are on the ballot in the November 3 midterm elections, and Democrats are hoping to take control of the chamber. Texas has long been seen as a Republican stronghold, and this race for the state’s open Senate seat is now one of the most closely watched in the nation. With the Bush family split out in the open, the final weeks of the campaign are set to draw even more national attention.
