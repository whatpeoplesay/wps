Senate Republicans plan post-election blitz to confirm Trump judicial nominees

Senate Republicans plan two judicial nomination hearings in November, after the midterms but before new members are sworn in. Democrats object to the break from usual practice.

16 October 2026 | Politics | By WPS Staff

Sources: Reuters, Nomination Notes (Punchbowl News reporting)

United States Senate Republicans are laying the groundwork to confirm as many of President Donald Trump's judicial nominees as possible in the weeks after the November 3 midterm elections. The push comes as the chance of Democrats flipping the chamber looms. Republicans now control the Senate with a 53-47 majority. If Democrats win control, the White House would face a harder road to confirm judicial nominees named by Trump.

Republican Senate Judiciary Committee Chair Chuck Grassley plans to hold two judicial nomination hearings during the Senate's two-week November session, after the election but before new members are sworn in. That breaks from the usual pattern of holding only one such hearing every two weeks. Six nominees are awaiting a hearing and could be eligible to appear at the November 10 hearing. Trump could also announce additional nominees who could go before the panel on November 18.

The back-to-back sessions would allow the panel to advance those nominees to the floor and have them voted on by the full Senate in December, during the final weeks of the current Congress. The nomination hearings were first reported by Punchbowl News. They were set over the objections of the panel's top Democrat, Senator Dick Durbin of Illinois.

Durbin warned that the move could be used against Republicans in the future under a Democratic president. He said the chairman's plan to hold two back-to-back nomination hearings one week apart without minority consent in November departs from longstanding committee practice. He said it establishes a new precedent that will allow Democrats to process nominees more quickly in a future Democratic administration.

Trump helped shift the judiciary's balance to the right during his first and second terms and secured a 6-3 conservative majority on the United States Supreme Court. He secured confirmation of 234 judicial nominees in his first term. Since Trump's return to the White House in January 2025, the Republican-led Senate has confirmed 56 judges, including 10 on the influential appeals courts. Among the six nominees awaiting hearings is Todd Butler, nominated to the New Orleans-based 5th US Circuit Court of Appeals. Another nominee, Samuel Adkisson of the White House Counsel's Office, is being held up by Senator Marsha Blackburn of Tennessee, who has declined without explanation to return a blue slip allowing his consideration for a federal judgeship in her home state.
