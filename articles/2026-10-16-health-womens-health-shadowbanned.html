Creators say women's health content is being shadowbanned on social media

Women's health creators and charities say their posts are removed, placed behind sensitivity filters, or shown to far fewer people. A new survey found 96 percent of those asked had faced some form of restriction.

16 October 2026 | Health | By WPS Staff

Sources: BBC News, LabourList (background on the CensHERship campaign findings)

Creators say women's health content is getting shadowbanned on social media. Shadowbanning means posts are removed, placed behind sensitivity filters that heavily blur them, or shown to far fewer people, without the poster being told clearly why.

Read More

One case involves Bella Moon, which sells pregnancy pillows. Founder Irene Breen said Instagram began removing adverts for the pillows in January 2025, and she was told the word pregnancy itself was the problem. She said the company had to remove the word from its website, change the product name, and rename web addresses containing it. The limits were lifted after 10 months of talks with Meta, and Breen said the business took a significant financial hit.

Campaigners say the case fits a broader pattern affecting creators and charities, not only advertisers. They say posts have been taken down, placed behind sensitivity filters, or shown to far fewer people. The campaign group CensHERship surveyed nearly 200 creators, clinicians, brands, charities and educators for a report last month, and 96 percent said they had faced some form of restriction.

The same survey found self-censorship rising, from 53 percent in 2025 to 65 percent this year, with people using coded spellings or substitute words, and avoiding some subjects altogether, to stay visible. Dr Carolina Are, a digital criminologist at the London School of Economics who studies social media moderation of women's content, said research shows artificial intelligence is often used to flag posts that might breach platform guidelines.

Meta, which owns Instagram and Facebook, declined to comment on individual cases. It told the BBC that adverts for wellness, sexual health and reproductive products are allowed, but restrictions exist to avoid negative experiences across countries and cultures.
