Slotkin calls for signature cases against Trump enablers and a deterrence message for young job seekers

Senator Elissa Slotkin said Democrats should pursue signature cases against people who enabled President Trump and send a deterrence message to young people considering Trump administration jobs.

19 October 2026 | Politics | By WPS Staff

Sources: Fox News, New York Post

Senator Elissa Slotkin of Michigan said Democrats should pursue signature cases against people who enabled President Donald Trump, not only against Trump himself. She spoke on October 13 at a University of Chicago event about her political path and the future of the Democratic Party. She said she wants the threat of prosecution to make young people think twice before taking jobs in the Trump administration.

Read More

Slotkin said Democrats must pick cases that are super egregious and go after people and projects in that group. She said the people around Trump who do his bidding have no immunity the way the president does. She said she wants young graduates to ask if a Trump administration job could buy them a ton of problems they do not want, and she called that a deterrence message.

A clip of the exchange was posted on October 17 by The Chicago Thinker, a student publication. The clip spread online and drew a fast backlash. Both Fox News and the New York Post reported that her remarks drew a firestorm on social media from conservatives.

Law professor Jonathan Turley wrote on X that Slotkin had said the quiet part out loud. Scott Kupor, the director of the Office of Personnel Management, called the comments disappointing and unprofessional and said his office believes in recruiting early career prospects. University of Chicago student Michael Bicksel wrote that Slotkin told him and other conservative students they should live in fear of political persecution for serving in the Trump administration.

Slotkin also said she voted to impeach Trump twice and noted that he stayed in the White House and survived four criminal cases against him. She said Democrats need accountability for what she called unbelievable corruption, but should not make that their whole personality. With the midterms approaching, her comments added to the debate over how Democrats would use congressional power if they win control of the House or Senate.
