Vance blames Iran war for Republican midterm losses

Vice President JD Vance said on Wednesday that the war with Iran was the reason Republicans lost control of Congress in the midterm elections, and he listed four issues voters wanted addressed.

4 November 2026 | US | By WPS Staff

Sources: USA Today, Fox News

Vice President JD Vance said on Wednesday morning, in his first public statement since the election, that the war with Iran was the reason Republicans lost the midterm elections. Writing on X, he said the president had said so the night before. Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate, flipping at least seven Senate seats and more than two dozen House districts.

Read More

Vance argued that the main problem was not that Republican supporters stayed home. He said many people who voted for the Trump-Vance ticket in 2024 voted for Democrats in 2026, and he pointed to a pollster who asked those voters what would bring them back to the party.

The pollster found four answers that covered nearly all of those voters, Vance said: end the war, focus on the cost of living and inflation, put America first, and change the relationship with Israel. He said any honest talk about what comes next must start with the war, and that anyone who thinks the cost was worth it, including losing the Senate for six years, should say so honestly.

The war began eight months ago, when the United States and Israel launched a joint attack against Iran, and it upended the world economy and pushed up fuel prices, according to the reports. It was deeply unpopular with American voters, and Republicans lost Senate races in deep-red states such as South Carolina, Texas and Kansas. President Donald Trump had said the war was part of the reason for the losses but that it was still justified.

Vance also warned against politicians who say amnesty is the answer to the party's problems, saying anyone pushing that idea was not telling the truth. He said he remained hopeful about the country's future, and he urged voters who were angry about the results to turn that anger into work to save the country.
