Waltz and Rodriguez clash in shouting match at UN over Cuba embargo

Washington's UN envoy Mike Waltz and Cuba's Foreign Minister Bruno Rodriguez clashed in a shouting match at the United Nations on October 27, during a debate on the decades-old US embargo on Cuba.

28 October 2026 | US | By WPS Staff

Sources: The National, ANews

Washington's UN envoy Mike Waltz and Cuba's Foreign Minister Bruno Rodriguez clashed in a shouting match at the United Nations General Assembly on Tuesday, October 27, during a debate on the US embargo on Cuba. The 193-member body was debating a resolution calling for an end to the trade embargo on the island, a measure it has voted for overwhelmingly almost every year since 1992.

The exchange began when Waltz accused Cuba's government of enriching its political elite while ordinary Cubans faced food shortages, power cuts and economic hardship. He also rejected Cuba's description of the US measures as a blockade, saying a blockade means a ring of military ships around an island, and that no such blockade exists.

Rodriguez repeatedly interrupted the speech, asking for Waltz to be called to order over what he described as offensive remarks against the Cuban government and its people. As the tension rose, Waltz told members of the Cuban delegation to move away from the podium, shouting: "I'm the representative of the United States of America. Back up. This isn't Havana. You're not going to intimidate me."

The President of the General Assembly, Khalilur Rahman, briefly suspended proceedings and then allowed Rodriguez to raise a point of order. Rodriguez accused Waltz of lying and of violating the dignity of the assembly, saying he was offending the dignity of the people and government of Cuba, and he interrupted again as Waltz showed photographs that he said showed the lavish lifestyles of Cuba's ruling elite.

The resolution itself is non-binding, and only the US Congress can lift the embargo, which has been in place for more than 60 years. Since the start of this year, the Trump administration has increased pressure on Havana by restricting oil shipments and threatening tariffs on countries that supply Cuba with fuel, measures that have worsened shortages and led to power cuts across the island, while Washington has blamed Cuba's economic troubles on its communist government rather than the embargo.
