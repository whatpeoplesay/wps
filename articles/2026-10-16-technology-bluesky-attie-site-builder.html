Bluesky expands Attie so anyone can build small sites and games

Bluesky expanded Attie, its experimental AI platform. Anyone can now build websites, quizzes, games, polls and other interactive experiences without writing code.

16 October 2026 | Technology | By WPS Staff

Sources: TechCrunch, Innovation Village

Bluesky has expanded Attie, its experimental AI platform, so anyone can build small social apps. People can now make websites, quizzes, games, polls and other interactive experiences. The company announced the change at TechCrunch Disrupt 2026 on Thursday.

Read More

Attie first offered AI technology for building custom social feeds. The new tools are meant to let everyday users make the kind of experiences that only software developers could build before. Each creation gets its own link and can be shared through individual website links.

The creations run on the AT Protocol, the same system that powers Bluesky and other decentralized social apps. Builders stay connected to their AT Protocol identity. Attie can also suggest things to build based on a user's public Bluesky profile.

Bluesky CEO Toni Schneider called the idea social vibe coding, or vibe coding for your community. He said vibe coding today is mostly a single-user activity, and Attie is meant to let people build together or build for a group. Users can bring their own AI model or use open-weight options.

Bluesky COO Rose Wang said Attie comes with Bluesky's distribution, so new creations start with an audience. The network has 47 million registered users, though engagement has slipped as some users went back to X. Support for private data is in alpha testing and should roll out by the end of the year.
