Miami edge Florida State 43-35 in double-overtime thriller after two disputed calls

No. 3 Miami stayed unbeaten with a 43-35 double-overtime win over Florida State on Saturday night. Two disputed fourth-down calls at the end decided the game, and the debate is still going.

19 October 2026 | Sports | By WPS Staff

Sources: USA TODAY, NBC Sports

No. 3 Miami beat Florida State 43-35 in double overtime on Saturday night at Hard Rock Stadium in Miami Gardens. The game ended with two disputed fourth-down calls that wiped out potential game-tying touchdowns for the Seminoles. Miami stayed unbeaten at 6-0, while the finish set off a wide debate over whether the officials got the calls right.

Miami scored first in the second overtime. Malachi Toney caught a 3-yard touchdown pass from Darian Mensah, then threw the two-point conversion to Cooper Barkate, putting the Hurricanes ahead 43-35. Florida State then faced fourth-and-21 from the Miami 36-yard line. Ashton Daniels threw deep to the end zone, and Micahi Danzy came down with the ball for what looked like a touchdown. But officials flagged Florida State lineman Xavier Chaplin for holding, which wiped out the score and pushed the Seminoles back 10 yards.

On fourth-and-31 from the Miami 46, Daniels rolled to his right and threw another high pass toward the end zone, aimed at Duce Robinson, the 6-foot-6 receiver. The ball dropped into a crowd of players. At first, nobody could tell who had it. After officials pulled the players apart, they ruled that Miami defensive back Bryce Fitzgerald had intercepted the pass. Officials reviewed the play with the ACC replay center, upheld the interception, and the game was over.

Florida State coach Mike Norvell said he was given no explanation for the interception call. He told reporters to go watch the film, and said that since the start of football, a tie has gone to the receiver. Daniels said he thought the play was a touchdown, but added that players do not control the decisions. ESPN rules analyst Steve Shaw backed the final call, saying the defender clearly had his hands on the ball and there was nothing to overturn. Former official Terry McAulay argued the holding call should not have been made.

The finish capped a wild night. Miami rallied from seven points down to lead 28-21 with 2:49 left in regulation, before Daniels found Robinson for a 75-yard touchdown that sent the game to overtime. Florida State led 35-28 in the first overtime on a 20-yard touchdown catch by Robinson, and Miami answered with a 25-yard rushing touchdown by Javian Mallory, who ran for 108 yards on 13 carries. Mensah finished with 326 passing yards and three touchdowns. With top-ranked Georgia and No. 2 Notre Dame losing the same day, Miami is now the highest-ranked unbeaten team left, in line for the No. 1 ranking. Coach Mario Cristobal played that down, saying the ranking has no value to his team's process.
