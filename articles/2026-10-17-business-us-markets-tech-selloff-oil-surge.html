US stocks fall again as tech sell-off deepens and oil prices surge

Wall Street closed lower on Friday as a sell-off in technology shares extended and oil prices climbed, capping the market's worst week since late June.

17 October 2026 | Business | By WPS Staff

Sources: The Manila Times, CNBC TV18 (via TradingView)

A broad sell-off in technology shares pulled Wall Street lower on Friday, capping the market's worst week since late June. The S&P 500 fell 1.1 percent, the Nasdaq composite sank 2.1 percent, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average held up better, closing 0.3 percent lower. The Nasdaq posted its worst weekly drop since June, and the S&P 500 gave back its gains from the week before, when it set an all-time high.

Chipmakers led the decline. Micron Technology fell 5.6 percent, Advanced Micro Devices dropped 6 percent, Broadcom lost 4.5 percent, and Intel gave up 5.8 percent. Meta fell 3.7 percent and Google parent Alphabet dropped 2.5 percent. Chipmakers were down for an eighth straight day, their longest losing streak since 2008. CoreWeave, a company at the center of the recent sell-off, fell another 11 percent on Friday, taking its loss for the week to 25 percent, while its total debt has risen to 51 billion dollars.

Investors are worried about how long the rush to build artificial intelligence systems can last. Many of the companies at the center of the boom have relied on debt markets to fund heavy spending, and yields in bond markets around the world have recently climbed to their highest levels in years. The debate has shifted from how large artificial intelligence can become to how much more investment will be needed before it turns a profit. Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang said the technology will keep growing and will affect every industry and every country in a positive way.

Oil prices stayed high and added to the pressure. Brent crude, the international standard, jumped 3.7 percent to settle at 109.86 dollars a barrel, up from around 72 dollars a barrel in late February before the start of the Iran war. Energy shares held up better than most, with Exxon rising 2.5 percent and Chevron adding 2.6 percent. The national average for gasoline stood at 4.33 dollars a gallon on Friday, lower than the 4.37 dollars a gallon a month earlier but sharply higher than the 3.06 dollars a gallon a year ago.

In the bond market, the yield on the 10-year Treasury slipped to 5.09 percent from 5.10 percent late Thursday, after topping 5.30 percent earlier this month. Reports this week showed inflation at the consumer and wholesale level rose more than economists expected, and traders now see a 74.3 percent chance of a rate increase at the Federal Reserve's December meeting, up from 50.2 percent a month ago. Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh offered no fresh signals on interest rate policy while attending a meeting of financial leaders in Bangkok. Elsewhere, Netflix fell 2.9 percent a day after announcing a change of chief content officer and plans to cut about 6 percent of its workforce, while Tesla fell 2.1 percent after unveiling a new Roadster model. European and Asian markets closed higher.
