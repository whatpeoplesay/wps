Bank of America profit misses estimates after $1.2 billion regulatory charge

Bank of America missed third-quarter profit estimates on October 14. Weaker trading and a $1.2 billion charge tied to a regulatory penalty over failures to detect potential money laundering drove the miss.

16 October 2026 | Business | By WPS Staff

Sources: Reuters via SRN News, The Business Times

Bank of America missed third-quarter profit estimates on Wednesday due to weakness in its trading business. The bank also disclosed a $1.2 billion charge tied to a regulatory penalty over failures to detect potential money-laundering activity. Shares of the second-biggest US lender fell more than 8 percent, hitting their lowest price since May.

The bank's sales and trading revenue fell 5 percent to $5.1 billion in the quarter, as weakness in fixed income, currencies and commodities overshadowed record quarterly equities trading. The bank had last month projected quarterly sales and trading revenue to be relatively flat compared to last year. Its investment banking fees fell 11 percent to $1.8 billion in the quarter, as dealmaking slowed.

The fine, unveiled late Tuesday, stems from the bank's failure to report over $5 billion in suspicious activity to the US government. The actions include the third-largest penalty in US Treasury history. Regulators have placed the bank under a consent order that requires the bank to work with a third party to identify and report suspicious transactions that went undetected. The bank had added thousands of people to shore up its efforts to tackle money laundering.

The Charlotte, North Carolina-based bank reported a profit of $6.9 billion, or 91 cents per share, for the three months ended September 30, compared with $8.3 billion, or $1.04 per share, a year earlier. Excluding the $1.2 billion regulatory settlement charge, the bank earned $1.08 per share, missing estimates of $1.10 per share, according to estimates compiled by LSEG.

In a bright spot, net interest income, the difference between what a bank earns on loans and pays out on deposits, jumped 8 percent in the quarter, driven by higher loan and deposit balances and fixed-rate asset repricing. The bank expects interest income to improve sequentially in the fourth quarter. Full-year net interest income growth is anticipated to be 8 percent or more in 2027.
