Trump cancels Kansas and Iowa rallies, switches to tele-rallies

President Donald Trump has called off his final weekend campaign rallies in Kansas and Iowa and will instead phone in to virtual events across nearly 20 states before Tuesday's midterm elections.

30 October 2026 | US | By WPS Staff

Sources: Reuters, Al Jazeera

US President Donald Trump has cancelled his planned weekend campaign rallies in Kansas and Iowa, days before the November 3 midterm elections. The Republican National Committee confirmed the change on Thursday, saying the president will instead take part in a series of virtual "tele-rallies" in nearly 20 states.

Read More

The cancelled in-person events were set for Salina, Kansas, on Saturday and Dubuque, Iowa, on Sunday. Trump told reporters he did not feel he had to go to Kansas, saying Republican Senator Roger Marshall, who faces a tight race against Democrat Adam Hamilton, was doing very well. A CNN poll released on Thursday put Hamilton ahead by four points, 49 percent to 45 percent.

Since October 1, Trump has held 25 rallies in 19 states, often speaking for more than an hour at each stop. His final in-person rally before Election Day will now be in Daytona Beach, Florida, on Friday. After that, the MAGA Inc campaign group said it will host tele-rallies with the president in at least 19 states, including Kansas and Iowa.

Republican National Committee chairman Joe Gruters said the virtual events will reach more voters than the cancelled stops could, calling the closing stretch a 72-hour sprint. The November 3 elections will decide control of Congress, where Republicans now hold 53 of 100 Senate seats and 218 of 435 House seats.

Trump has also spoken about the strain of the schedule, saying in Mississippi on Monday that no one's vocal cords were made for a rally every day. On Thursday, after campaigning in South Carolina, he brushed aside a reporter's question about whether he was tired of travelling, saying he felt perfect.
