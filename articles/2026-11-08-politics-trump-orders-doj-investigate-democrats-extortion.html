Trump orders Justice Department to investigate Democrats over "extortion" claims

Donald Trump has ordered the Justice Department to look into Democrats, accusing them of threatening people with impeachment and other punishment after their midterm election win.

8 November 2026 | Politics | By WPS Staff

Sources: Al Jazeera, CNA

Donald Trump has ordered the United States Department of Justice to investigate Democrats, saying they have been threatening people with impeachment and other forms of punishment. In a post on Truth Social on Saturday, he called what Democrats had done a "criminal act" and "extortion at the highest level". He gave no evidence for the claim.

Read More

The post came days after Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress from Republicans in Tuesday's midterm elections. They will take control in January, and Democratic lawmakers have said they plan to investigate the Trump family's financial dealings, immigration enforcement, and the defence secretary and the attorney general. Robert Garcia, a Democratic representative from California who is likely to lead the House Oversight Committee, posted "LAWYER UP" on social media after the result became clear.

Trump wrote that Democrats were going around threatening a lot of people, and that some members of his cabinet had been threatened with impeachment. He also said, without evidence, that Democrats were threatening his donors, whom he called some of the most respected people anywhere in the world, because of their political views. He named Michigan Senator Elissa Slotkin, but gave no evidence that she had threatened people over their views.

Trump called for prison time for "these extortionists" and said those responsible should be held accountable. The order came a day after he named Stephen Miller as his next chief of staff, replacing Susie Wiles. His combative tone echoed comments he made after a smaller midterm defeat in 2018, when he said his White House would take a "war-like posture" against investigations. He was later impeached in 2019 and 2021.

Once Democrats take control of Congress, they will be able to order access to documents, summon Trump officials to testify, and hold public hearings. A third impeachment of Trump is possible, though party leaders have not promised it. Removing a president is a much bigger step, because it needs a two-thirds vote in the Senate, far beyond the Democrats' majority.
