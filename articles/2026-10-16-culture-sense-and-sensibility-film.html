New Sense and Sensibility puts sisterhood at the centre, stars say

A new Sense and Sensibility opens in theatres on Friday, 16 October. Daisy Edgar-Jones plays Elinor Dashwood and Esme Creed-Miles plays Marianne Dashwood.

16 October 2026 | Culture | By WPS Staff

Sources: Associated Press, via The Manila Times, Associated Press summary, via The Daily Reflector

A new Sense and Sensibility opens in theatres on Friday, 16 October. Daisy Edgar-Jones plays Elinor, the elder, more sensible Dashwood sister, and Esme Creed-Miles plays Marianne. The film is directed by Georgia Oakley, and it is the first major Sense and Sensibility to be directed by a woman.

Read More

Director Georgia Oakley said the film would live or die by the relationship between the two actors, and that finding sisters who were believably sisters was a pressurised task. For Edgar-Jones, the relationship between the sisters was kind of everything, and the search for the more outwardly romantic Dashwood was ongoing.

Creed-Miles and Edgar-Jones already knew each other. They had spent a formative summer in Yorkshire together as teenagers making an indie film called Pond Life and watching Love Island together at night. Edgar-Jones said so much of sisterhood is a shared history, a shared context, and a knowledge of one another, and that acting across from Creed-Miles, with an image of her 17-year-old face in mind, really helped. She said they just slotted straight into the roles.

Creed-Miles, whose parents are actors Samantha Morton and Charlie Creed-Miles, has been acting on and off for most of her life, starting with a role at age 6 playing a Shirley Temple impersonator in a Harmony Korine movie. She also starred in the Amazon Prime series Hanna, but has resisted committing to being a career actor. Her other pursuits include photography, music, and filmmaking. She was interested in Sense and Sensibility partly for the chance to work with Oakley, as a fan of her debut film Blue Jean, about a closeted gym teacher in England in the late 1980s.

To tell this story about a family of four women who have suddenly lost their fortune and now have to survive on their own, Oakley leaned into an earthier and slightly more undone look than is often shown in Regency-era films. She showed the cast Andrea Arnold's Wuthering Heights and Kelly Reichardt's Meek's Cutoff and made playlists full of violin music. The dresses she referenced had raspberry stains and soil on them. Oakley said she wanted a film where the characters did not feel at arm's length, and felt like human beings that viewers can reach out and touch through the screen. Creed-Miles said the film is for everyone, not just Austen fans, and that it might introduce a new generation to her characters.
