Sacha Baron Cohen’s Ali G comeback faces boycott campaign days before release

Ali G: Who Iz I? opens in theaters on October 23 as a 50,000-email boycott campaign urges cinemas not to screen it. The film’s co-writer says Ali G is “a tool to expose racists.”

21 October 2026 | Culture | By WPS Staff

Sources: NME, The A.V. Club

Sacha Baron Cohen is bringing Ali G back to the big screen with Ali G: Who Iz I?, opening in theaters on October 23. It is Cohen’s first film as a director, and it was shot in secret. But the comeback has sparked a fight. The media monitoring group NewsCord has sent more than 50,000 emails to 15 major cinema chains, urging them not to show the film. The group says the character is a cover for racist stereotypes. On the other side, the film’s co-writer Colton Dunn told Deadline that Ali G is not a racist character: “Ali G is a tool to expose racists.”

The campaign began in August, a day after the first trailer appeared. NewsCord says Cohen uses satire as a cover for enforcing racial stereotypes, pointing to the fake dreadlocks and Rastafarian-style hat in the trailer. The group has quoted old criticism going back to 2000, when Black British comedians called the character “an alibi for racism” and writer Jeanette Winterson compared the act to minstrel shows. One UK chain, Picturehouse, told the group it had no current plans to screen the film, though Variety noted that was a standard reply for titles that had not been scheduled yet.

Cohen answered the backlash in his own style. On October 7 he posted an Instagram skit in which he played a balding activist from a fictional Campaign for Ethical Comedy, urging people to boycott his own movie. On X he joked that in an age when a comedian’s career can be ended by a single un-PC joke, he had decided to play it safe and bring this character back. In an essay published by Time on October 20, he wrote that the fact Ali G is outdated and problematic is exactly why he brought the character back. He has also defended the film in a 60 Minutes interview.

The film follows Ali G, who has spent twenty years doing nothing, until he learns his long-time girlfriend Julie is eight months pregnant. Out of money, he heads to America to reclaim his old fame, but runs into enemies who threaten him and his child. A second trailer brought back two more Cohen characters: Borat, who tells viewers the film has been accused of racism but “sadly, it is not,” and Colonel Erran Morad. The world premiere took place on October 14 at the Hollywood Legion Theater in Los Angeles, where Cohen arrived in character in the yellow tracksuit and gold chain, alongside co-star Morgana Robinson.

Ali G first appeared in 1998 on Channel 4’s The 11 O’Clock Show, then moved to Da Ali G Show and the 2002 film Ali G Indahouse. Cohen retired the character in 2007. Since then he has built a career on provocative caricatures, from Borat to Bruno, saying the joke lands on the buffoons themselves and on the people they expose. Dunn, a Black writer known for Key & Peele and The Eric Andre Show, said Cohen puts himself in dangerous places while filming, and asked why he would do that if his intentions were racist. Critics, including comedians Gina Yashere, Mo Gilligan and Curtis Walker, and writer Afua Hirsch, argue the character lets audiences laugh at racial stereotypes. The debate now runs right up to opening weekend.
