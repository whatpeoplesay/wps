Trump attacks El-Sayed at Michigan rally and draws rebuke

President Donald Trump used a Port Huron rally to attack Michigan Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed, drawing a sharp rebuke from a former Arab American ally.

26 October 2026 | Politics | By WPS Staff

Sources: Associated Press, Al Jazeera

At a campaign rally in Port Huron, Michigan, on Sunday, President Donald Trump attacked Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed, calling him a jihadist and saying he would have to go through a metal detector before entering the Senate. Trump also compared stopping El-Sayed from entering the Senate with the passengers who fought back aboard United Flight 93 on September 11, 2001, and said he had come to Michigan to keep a jihadist Muslim Brotherhood member from becoming a senator.

El-Sayed, a former Detroit health official who was born in the United States, has denied any ties to extremist groups and has called attacks on his loyalty shameful. His campaign said he remains focused on plans to bring down costs and stand up to chaos and corruption in Washington. Bishara Bahbah, a founder of Arab Americans for Trump, said the president's statements were out of line, crossing a red line, and racist.

The Michigan race is for the open seat of retiring Democratic Senator Gary Peters, and it is one of a handful of contests that could decide control of the Senate. El-Sayed faces Republican Mike Rogers, who also took the stage at the Port Huron rally and repeated the claim linking El-Sayed to the Muslim Brotherhood. Republicans now hold a 53 seat majority in the 100 member Senate, and only 35 Senate seats are up for election in 2026.

Trump also warned that if El-Sayed and the Democrats won, they would open the floodgates and invite jihadists to American shores, creating a nightmare of violence and terror until people could not even have a Christmas. He also attacked Democratic Representative Ilhan Omar of Minnesota at the same rally. On the war on Iran, which has pushed up fuel prices, Trump told the crowd he had no regrets about the decision and would not second guess it.

The crowd at the McMorran Place arena cheered through the speech, and rapper Nicki Minaj appeared on stage and urged people to vote for the Republican ticket. With the midterm election set for November 3, Michigan remains one of the closest watched battlegrounds in the fight for the Senate.
