Record rain floods Istanbul and diverts flights

Torrential rain caused chaos across Istanbul as the city recorded its highest rainfall in more than 80 years. Flights were diverted and cancelled, schools closed, and residents were told to stay home unless travel was absolutely necessary.

16 October 2026 | Travel | By WPS Staff

Sources: The National, Gulf News

Torrential rainfall caused chaos across Istanbul as the city recorded its highest rainfall in more than 80 years. Authorities reported three deaths linked to the flooding.

Read More

Istanbul Governor Davut Gul told the city 16 million residents to stay at home unless outings were absolutely necessary. He closed schools and banned motorbikes as 8,000 emergency service workers were sent out to deal with the effects of days of downpours. He said rainfall in Istanbul will continue for another two to three days.

Many areas of the city had received more than 100mm of rain since early on Wednesday, Istanbul Metropolitan Municipality said. Rainfall in the Beykoz area of north-eastern Istanbul had reached 242mm. About 3,000 people were rescued from flooded areas, the governor said.

At Istanbul Airport, 51 flights were diverted to alternative airports and another 19 flights were cancelled, Mr Gul said. Emergency services received more than 1,200 reports of flooding, 15 of landslides, 47 of people trapped and two of roofs blown off, the governorship said.

Residents received text messages from disaster management services on Wednesday evening warning them to stay away from riverbanks and flooded roads. Residents of basement apartments were told they should take precautions, while power cuts halted water supplies to homes and businesses in parts of the city. Heavy flooding also made streets impassable in the city of Antalya on Mediterranean coast of Turkey.
