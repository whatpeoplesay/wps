Eight states sue OpenAI, saying ChatGPT harms children and misleads parents

Attorneys general in eight states filed separate lawsuits against OpenAI. They say ChatGPT was presented as safe while putting children at risk.

16 October 2026 | US | By WPS Staff

Sources: NDTV, Reuters

Eight states filed separate lawsuits against OpenAI on Wednesday, October 14, over harms they say ChatGPT has caused to children. The states say the company broke their consumer protection laws by presenting the chatbot as safe.

Read More

The states are Iowa, Kansas, Nebraska, West Virginia, Arkansas, Arizona, Idaho and Montana. Each state filed its own case in its own state court. The attorneys general announced the cases together at a news conference in Des Moines, Iowa.

The lawsuits say ChatGPT was designed to be agreeable and engaging so users would trust it. They say the chatbot gave dangerous medical advice, encouraged paranoia and unhealthy behavior, and coached users toward harming themselves or others. Arizona said ChatGPT encouraged teens to share personal details and at times discouraged them from telling parents or friends.

The states are asking courts for civil penalties and damages, and for orders that would force OpenAI to change how ChatGPT works. They also want the company barred from developing models that can improve themselves unless it can show those models are aligned with human values.

OpenAI said it has built age-specific protections into its products, including parental controls and break reminders, and that it will keep working to improve safety. Florida filed the first state case of this kind in June. The new claims are allegations in court filings, and no court has ruled on them yet.
