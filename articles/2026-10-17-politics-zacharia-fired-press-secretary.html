Katie Zacharia out as White House press secretary after less than a week

White House press secretary Katie Zacharia was fired on Friday after less than a week on the job, in what is believed to be the shortest tenure of any press secretary.

17 October 2026 | Politics | By WPS Staff

Sources: Reuters, The Japan Times

White House press secretary Katie Zacharia was fired on Friday after less than a week on the job, in what is believed to be the shortest tenure of any press secretary. Two White House officials said she was let go and is transitioning out of the role. Her exit leaves President Donald Trump without a key public messenger just over two weeks before November's midterm elections.

Read More

Zacharia started on Monday and held her debut White House briefing on Tuesday. The briefing lasted about 14 minutes, and she took questions from only three reporters, from Breitbart, Fox News and the Daily Mail. She had received a call about the job on October 7 and was offered the role to succeed Karoline Leavitt, who left at the end of August.

Questions soon followed about her career. She said she had practiced international transaction law with all the appropriate credentials, and Trump had also described her as an attorney. Reuters could not find records showing the Pepperdine University law school graduate was registered as an attorney or a member of the bar in any American state or the District of Columbia.

During a Fox News interview on Thursday, Zacharia said she did not pass the California bar exam. Law school graduates cannot legally practice law in the United States without first being admitted to a state bar. Her debut briefing and her interviews also drew criticism over her performance.

Earlier on Friday, she told reporters at the White House that she was looking forward to working with the press. Trump told reporters later that day that he had asked her to reconsider whether to continue, saying he thought she would be better off being happy. In a phone interview with Fox on Friday, Trump said he was in no rush to name a new press secretary. Anthony Scaramucci, who served 10 days as communications director in Trump's first administration, said records are made to be broken, but he took no pleasure in other people's pain.
