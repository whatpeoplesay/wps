China and North Korea open long-delayed Yalu River bridge

China and North Korea opened the New Yalu River Bridge after a wait of more than 12 years. Trucks crossed between Dandong and Sinuiju at the opening ceremony.

16 October 2026 | World | By WPS Staff

Sources: Channel News Asia, Al Jazeera

China and North Korea opened the New Yalu River Bridge on Thursday, ending a wait of more than 12 years. The 3 kilometre bridge links the Chinese city of Dandong with the North Korean city of Sinuiju.

Read More

The bridge was finished in 2014 but stayed unused because roads and customs facilities on the North Korean side were not ready. Trucks carrying the flags of both countries drove across at the opening ceremony, while crowds watched from the Chinese side.

Chinese Premier Li Qiang said in a video address that relations were entering a new phase and a new chapter. He said the crossing would strongly promote economic and trade cooperation and closer exchanges between people on both sides. North Korean Premier Pak Thae Song also gave a video address, although North Korean state media had not yet reported on the event.

The opening is the latest sign of warming ties between Beijing and Pyongyang after two summits between their leaders in the past year. Analysts said the bridge is unlikely to bring an immediate return to trade levels from before sanctions, but it will help move heavy equipment, raw materials and energy goods that North Korea needs.

China is by far North Korea's largest trading partner, even after United Nations sanctions over its missile and nuclear weapons work. Until now, most goods crossed about 10 kilometres upstream on an 83 year old bridge with a rail track and a single lane road. Chinese exports to North Korea rose to 2.3 billion dollars last year, a six year high, but still well below levels from before sanctions tightened in 2017.
