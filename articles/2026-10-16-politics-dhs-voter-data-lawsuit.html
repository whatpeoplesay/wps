Lawsuit says DHS effort to collect voter data from 47 states breaks federal law

A lawsuit filed Thursday says the Trump administration obtained voter files from at least 47 states. The groups behind it ask the court to declare the collection illegal and order the data deleted.

16 October 2026 | Politics | By WPS Staff

Sources: Associated Press, USA TODAY

A lawsuit filed Thursday challenges the Trump administration's effort to collect and analyze Americans' voter data. The suit alleges the government has obtained voter files from at least 47 states and handled the data in a way that violates federal law. It was filed in federal court in Washington, D.C.

Read More

The 42-page complaint claims the Department of Homeland Security's probe of the voter data, including a policy to combine it with commercial datasets, disregards federal safeguards and flouts the privacy rights of millions of Americans. The case was brought by the League of Women Voters, the Electronic Privacy Information Center, and the League of United Latin American Citizens. The Department of Homeland Security did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The case is the latest in a series of legal challenges to President Donald Trump's push against noncitizen voting. Trump views noncitizen voting as a serious problem, but research shows it is exceedingly rare. The administration has sought more power over elections, even as the Constitution grants that authority to states and, in some cases, to Congress. Judges have repeatedly upheld states' rights to decline to hand over their voter lists to the federal government.

The lawsuit alleges the administration has obtained voter lists from 47 states, even as 30 states refused to turn them over. It calls for the court to declare the administration's collection of voter data illegal, block the policy, and order the department to delete the information it has already collected and stop using it. The lawsuit comes less than three weeks before Election Day, as the administration has kept up pressure on states over removing noncitizens from their voter rolls.

On the same day, the administration sued California election officials, alleging they did not act quickly enough to review names the federal government identified as likely noncitizens. The Justice Department asked a judge to bar the state from counting those people's mail ballots for next month's midterm elections. The California secretary of state's office said it was given just 48 hours to review 20,000 names, without the Justice Department providing any evidence to back up its claims.
