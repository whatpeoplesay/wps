Four killed as Indonesia battles worst forest fires in over a decade

Four people tackling a wildfire in a national park on Indonesia's island of Java were killed. The country is battling its worst forest fires in more than a decade.

16 October 2026 | World | By WPS Staff

Sources: Al Jazeera, Reuters

Four people tackling a wildfire in a national park on Indonesia's island of Java were killed, the Forestry Ministry said on Friday. The country is battling its worst forest fires in more than a decade.

Read More

The victims were caught in the flames on Thursday in Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park in East Java. Two other people suffered serious burns and were receiving medical treatment. The ministry did not identify the victims.

In a statement, the ministry paid tribute to the courage and dedication of officers, volunteers and residents working on the fire lines. It said the deaths were a reminder that putting out forest and land fires is a high risk operation, and that safety must come first, including tracking how a fire spreads and the wind direction, keeping evacuation routes ready, and checking conditions before and during operations.

The islands of Sumatra and Borneo, close to Singapore and Malaysia, are the worst affected. Satellite monitoring found 799 high confidence hotspots across the country as of Thursday night, up 427 from the day before. The ministry cautioned that hotspots are not the same as fires, since one blaze can register as several hotspots.

President Prabowo Subianto has approved accepting help from neighbouring countries, Foreign Minister Arrmanatha Nasir said. Malaysia offered last month to send three helicopters, an aircraft and 52 personnel, but there has been no follow-up. Singapore has also said it is ready to help. The fires have caused breathing problems for hundreds of thousands of people and forced school closures in Malaysia and East Timor, as haze spread beyond Indonesia's borders.
