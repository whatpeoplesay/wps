LeBron James booed by Knicks fans in 76ers debut as MSG chants “we don’t want you”

Knicks fans greeted LeBron James with “we don’t want you” chants in his Philadelphia 76ers debut on Tuesday night, but the 41-year-old got the last word as the Sixers beat the defending champions 106-100 on their banner night.

21 October 2026 | Sports | By WPS Staff

Sources: Sportsnet, People

LeBron James, 41, opened his 24th NBA season in a Philadelphia 76ers jersey on Tuesday night at Madison Square Garden, and the home crowd gave him a harsh welcome. When he stepped to the free-throw line early in the game, Knicks fans erupted with chants of “we don’t want you.” The Sixers beat the defending champions 106-100, spoiling the Knicks’ championship banner and ring night.

Read More

The chants had a backstory. James said over the summer that the Knicks would have been his first choice in free agency if they had not won their first NBA title since 1973. He told ESPN he had visions of finishing his career at the Garden, “one of the best places I’ve ever played,” but changed his mind after New York beat San Antonio in the Finals. He then signed a two-year, $8 million deal with the 76ers.

The night had more than chants. Near the end of the first half, James exchanged heated words with a courtside fan who seemed to tell him to go back to the West Coast. “I’m not from the West Coast. I’m from Ohio,” James answered. Spike Lee, a famous Knicks fan, stepped between the two as a peacemaker. James also had a jab for the referees, caught on camera saying, “They win one chip, they get a championship whistle.”

On the court, James finished with 14 points, nine rebounds and seven assists in a mostly facilitating role. Tyrese Maxey led Philadelphia with 28 points and five assists, Jaylen Brown added 19, and Joel Embiid had 13 points and 11 rebounds. The Knicks led 58-54 at halftime, but the Sixers outscored them 29-18 in the third quarter, with James setting up Brown for a buzzer-beating three. Maxey’s late steal and layup helped seal the win, snapping a streak of four straight losses in James’s debuts with new teams.

James refused to call it a spoiled party. “It was a great night for New York fans. Fifty-plus years,” he said, adding that the fans will never forget the moment after ending a long championship drought, like he did in Cleveland. James will face the Garden crowd again on Feb. 27, and the 76ers hope this is the start of a season that ends with their own banner night.
