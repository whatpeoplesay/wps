Ramaswamy ad with Acton's estranged mother splits Ohio GOP

An ad from Vivek Ramaswamy's campaign puts Amy Acton's estranged mother on camera calling her a liar. Ohio Republicans are split over it, with Trump defending the spot.

24 October 2026 | Politics | By WPS Staff

Sources: USA Today, CNN

Ohio's governor race has a new flashpoint. A campaign ad from Republican Vivek Ramaswamy features his Democratic rival's estranged mother on camera, calling Amy Acton a liar about her past and telling Ohioans not to vote for her. The ad never aired on TV, but it spread fast on social media this week and has now split the state's Republican Party.

The backlash centers on Acton's family history. She has had no contact with her mother, Donna Stearns Arthur, since she was 12. In 1978, her stepfather Cledith Arthur was charged with sexually abusing her, and records reviewed by USA TODAY back Acton's account. Cledith died without being convicted, and Arthur stayed married to him until his death. Acton called the ad "awful." Her brother Phillip Stearns called it a "desperate, disgusting attack," saying Ramaswamy is "a pathetic excuse for a man" willing to hurt anyone to get what he wants.

The criticism came from inside Ramaswamy's own party. Governor Mike DeWine called the ad "deeply, deeply troubling" and "just wrong." Former governor John Kasich called it "completely inappropriate." Senator Jon Husted said he would not have run the spot, and Secretary of State Frank LaRose said he probably would not run one like it.

President Trump went the other way. He called the ad "very powerful" and said using it was "hitting pay dirt." He attacked DeWine as a RINO who is "disloyal to the Republican Party." Vice President JD Vance told Republicans criticizing the ad to "stop whining." Ramaswamy confirmed the ad came from his campaign and said Acton's mother was not paid to appear in it.

The race is one of the most watched of the midterms, with weeks to go before Election Day. The Real Clear Politics polling average has Acton ahead by 3.6 percentage points. The clip first surfaced online on Oct. 19 through Ohio politics blogger The Rooster, and it has stayed at the center of the race ever since.
