Airport self-checkout asks passenger to tip 18% for a water bottle

A viral TikTok from an airport self-service store has sparked a fresh debate over tipping culture, after a payment kiosk prompted an 18% tip for a $6.49 water bottle with no workers in sight.

22 October 2026 | Travel | By WPS Staff

Sources: New York Post, The Sun

Dr. Clay Moss, a longevity specialist, posted the video on TikTok over the weekend from a fully self-service store at an airport he did not name. When he scanned a bottle of water priced at $6.49, the self-checkout screen prompted him to leave an 18% tip. He switched it to zero, then panned the camera around the store to show there was not a single worker in sight.

On camera, he asked who the tip was even for. “Who is this automatic 18% tip that I have to click out of for? Who am I tipping here? Is this what we’ve come to?” he said in the video.

In his caption, Moss wrote that tips normally go to store staff such as cashiers or attendants. But the kiosk is owned and operated by a third party, so it is unclear where the tip money would end up. When a third-party payment system is used, the tip is routed through that system to the vendor, and policies on distribution vary by company.

The video went viral over the weekend and was covered by the press on October 21, 2026. TikTok users flooded the video with their takes. One suggested that customers who do the checkout work themselves should get a discount. Others turned on the price itself, asking why a bottle of water costs $6.49 in the first place.

Tipping prompts are now showing up in more places than a few years ago. Tipping is the norm at restaurants, bars and salons, but the kiosk added it at a store where the customer does all the work. Whether the trend has gone too far is the argument now playing out online.
