US measles cases top 4,500, more than double 2025 total

The CDC has reported 4,588 measles cases in the United States in 2026, more than double the total for all of 2025, as the outbreak spreads across 43 outbreaks and Pennsylvania remains the hardest-hit state.

27 October 2026 | Health | By WPS Staff

Sources: Reuters, CDC

The United States has recorded 4,588 confirmed measles cases so far in 2026, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, more than double the 2,289 cases reported in all of 2025. The latest weekly update, current as of October 22, added 178 cases in a week. The rise continues the worst resurgence of the disease in the country in decades, after measles was declared eliminated in the United States in 2000.

Children and teenagers aged 5 to 19 make up the largest group of cases, with 1,914 infections, or 42 percent of the total, CDC data show. Adults aged 20 and older account for 1,881 cases, or 41 percent, while children under 5 account for 766 cases. Hospitalizations this year stand at 479, about 10 percent of all cases. The CDC says 96 percent of confirmed cases were in people who were unvaccinated or whose vaccination status was unknown, and 96 percent were linked to outbreaks, with 43 new outbreaks reported in 2026.

Pennsylvania remains the centre of the surge. The state health department reported 1,317 cases as of October 21, with 251 hospitalizations, according to Reuters. Pennsylvania has reported five measles-related deaths so far in 2026, while the CDC has confirmed three measles-associated deaths nationally. The CDC previously said it would send additional support to Pennsylvania to help investigate the outbreak.

Health officials point to vaccination as the main protection. The CDC says two doses of the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine are 97 percent effective at preventing measles, and one dose is 93 percent effective. Vaccination coverage among US kindergartners has fallen from 95.2 percent in the 2019 to 2020 school year to 92.4 percent in the 2025 to 2026 school year, leaving about 280,000 kindergartners at risk, according to the CDC. Measles spreads through the air when an infected person coughs or sneezes, and a person can catch it simply by being in a room where someone with measles has been.

The CDC notes that its case counts are preliminary and can change as reviews are completed. State, local and tribal health departments lead measles investigations in their areas and hold the most up to date local information. The agency is also working with state epidemiologists to develop a standard definition for measles deaths, so that states and the federal government classify deaths in the same way.
