Paraguay president removed from office, placed under house arrest after bank collapse

Paraguay's Senate voted to remove President Santiago Pena from office on Friday, and prosecutors placed him under house arrest hours later, after the collapse of a bank that locked nearly half the country out of their savings.

7 November 2026 | World | By WPS Staff

Sources: Associated Press, Reuters

Paraguay's Senate voted on Friday to remove President Santiago Pena from office, and prosecutors placed him under house arrest shortly after, following the collapse of a bank that left nearly half the country without access to their savings. All 44 senators present voted to remove him for misconduct in office.

The crisis began three days earlier, when regulators took over ueno bank, saying it had failed to meet minimum solvency requirements. The move locked about three million customers, nearly half of Paraguay's more than six million people, out of their savings and bank cards. It set off the country's worst political crisis in more than a decade.

Pena had submitted his resignation on Thursday, but the Senate went ahead with the vote. Lawmakers accused him of using his political influence to steer hundreds of millions of dollars in public funds into ueno while regulators overlooked risky insider lending. The charges also pointed to his past stake in the company that owned the bank, which he divested last year, and his appointment of a former ueno executive, Carlos Carvallo, to run the central bank. Pena has denied wrongdoing.

Prosecutors ordered Pena to stay at his home in Asuncion until he appears before their economic crimes unit on Saturday. The charges include breach of trust, influence peddling and using public office for personal gain, the most serious of which carry up to 10 years in prison. Prosecutors also ordered the arrest of Carvallo. As senators debated on Friday, protesters marched through downtown Asuncion demanding their frozen savings back, chanting 'Santi Pena to Tacumbu!', a reference to the capital's main prison, where ueno's founder Miguel Vazquez is being held.

Vice President Pedro Alliana was sworn in as president on Friday and will serve out the term, which ends in August 2028. Pena is the second Paraguayan president removed by impeachment since the country returned to democracy in 1989. In a farewell message before the vote, Pena wrote that he did not want his presence "to be a cause of greater division or instability" and said goodbye "with gratitude, without ignoring the anguish of those who are waiting for answers today."
