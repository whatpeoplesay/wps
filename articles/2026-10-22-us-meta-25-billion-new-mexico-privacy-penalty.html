Meta Ordered to Pay $25 Billion to New Mexico in Privacy Case

A New Mexico judge ordered Meta to pay $25 billion after a jury found the company misled Facebook users about privacy, one of the largest corporate penalties in US history. Meta says it will appeal.

22 October 2026 | US | By WPS Staff

Sources: CNN, USA Today

A New Mexico judge ordered Meta to pay $25 billion on Wednesday after the company was found to have misled Facebook users about their privacy. It is one of the largest penalties ever handed to a company in the United States.

Read More

The case goes back to the Cambridge Analytica scandal. Facebook disclosed in 2018 that the data of as many as 87 million users was taken by the political consulting firm, which used it to build profiles of American voters. New Mexico sued the company in 2021, saying it had deceived state residents about who could see their personal information.

In September, a jury found that Meta had broken the state’s consumer protection law more than 43 million times. State law allows a fine of up to $5,000 per violation, so the judge found that a penalty of more than $219 billion was warranted before settling on $25 billion. In his order, Judge Francis Mathew wrote that Facebook’s deception was not a set of isolated mistakes but a systematic pattern at the core of its business.

Meta said it will appeal. Its chief legal officer, C.J. Mahoney, said the ruling “makes it all but impossible to do business in the state of New Mexico” and that the case “seeks to impose a tax on speech to enforce new rules for what people can say online.” He said the company is confident it will win on appeal.

New Mexico attorney general Raul Torrez called the penalty one of the largest against a corporation in US history and a warning to large technology companies. Money from such fines goes to state education. The state’s lawyers had asked for between $35 billion and $40 billion. Meta previously faced a $5 billion federal fine over the same scandal in 2019.
