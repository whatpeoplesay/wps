Musk pledges one SpaceX share to every US child as SpaceX posts first profit since IPO

SpaceX reported its first profitable quarter since its June IPO as Q3 2026 revenue jumped 161% to $14.58 billion, with AI sales overtaking Starlink for the first time. A day earlier, Elon Musk pledged one SpaceX share to every American child.

21 October 2026 | Business | By WPS Staff

Sources: TipRanks, TradingView/Stocktwits, MarketWatch, FXLeaders

On Monday, October 19, Elon Musk said he would give one of his SpaceX shares to every child in America in 2027. Writing on X, he said: "Next year, I will give one of my SpaceX shares to every kid in America. This will be the first of many." President Donald Trump said the shares would be distributed through Trump Accounts, tax-advantaged accounts for American minors. The pledge came after AI company Anthropic said it would commit 5 percent of its shares to a public-benefit fund. Musk replied that companies should give equity directly to the public instead. About 73 million children under 18 live in the United States, and at a share price near $148, the gift would be worth over $10 billion. The shares would come from Musk’s own holdings; an August filing showed he owns 4.76 billion SpaceX shares.

The pledge landed a day before SpaceX reported its third-quarter 2026 results on Tuesday, October 20. Revenue reached $14.58 billion, up 161 percent from a year earlier and above analyst estimates of about $13 billion. Earnings per share were $0.35, beating the expected $0.13. It was the company’s first profitable quarter since it listed on the Nasdaq in June, with net income of about $4.4 billion after a $541 million loss in the second quarter.

The strongest growth came from the company’s artificial intelligence business, now called Super Intelligence. AI revenue reached $7.56 billion and passed the Starlink connectivity business for the first time. The jump came from new cloud-computing deals, the purchase of software company Cursor in August, and wider use of the Grok AI model. The AI unit posted $431 million in operating income after a $1.26 billion operating loss the quarter before.

The Starlink side kept growing too. Connectivity revenue rose 69 percent to $5.25 billion, and operating income nearly doubled to $2.37 billion. Starlink ended the quarter with 13.6 million consumer subscribers, up from 7.2 million a year earlier, although the average monthly payment per subscriber fell from $83 to $69. The space launch business brought in $1.77 billion, and its operating loss shrank to a fraction of the quarter before.

Growth came at a price. Capital spending reached $19.6 billion in the quarter, mostly on AI infrastructure, leaving free cash flow negative. SpaceX ended the quarter with about $89.9 billion in cash and is now valued near $2 trillion. The stock rose in after-hours trading on Tuesday. More shares hit the market soon: up to 1.3 billion shares become tradable on Thursday, October 22, the largest single release since the June IPO, with another 328 million unlocking on October 24.
