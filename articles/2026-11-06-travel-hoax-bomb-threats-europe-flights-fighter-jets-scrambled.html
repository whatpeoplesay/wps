Hoax bomb threats hit European flights for third day as fighter jets scramble

More than 20 commercial flights across Europe were hit by hoax bomb threats on Thursday, for a third day this week, as fighter jets scrambled to escort planes and several flights diverted.

6 November 2026 | Travel | By WPS Staff

Sources: AFP via Malay Mail, Dawn

More than 20 commercial flights across Europe were hit by hoax bomb threats on Thursday, for a third day this week. Fighter jets from Italy, France and Bulgaria scrambled to escort planes, and several flights were diverted or forced to land away from their planned airports. Every targeted plane landed safely, and searches found no explosives.

At least five flights received an air force escort. Bulgaria's air force escorted three Ryanair planes heading for Sofia, from Madrid, Copenhagen and London, and all three were inspected, with no explosives found. French fighter jets escorted an Aer Lingus flight from Dublin to Rome and forced it to land at the Evreux military base, about 100 kilometres west of Paris, after the pilot had allegedly refused to land there and preferred a civilian airport.

Other flights were diverted too. A Ryanair flight from London to Sofia was sent to Plovdiv airport, and a Wizz Air flight from Milan to Warsaw was rerouted to Vienna. In Spain, three emailed bomb threats arrived in under 20 minutes, covering two Air Europa flights from Madrid to Paris and four inbound Iberia flights from Rome and Milan. In Warsaw, a LOT Polish Airlines flight to Paris was stopped as it moved towards take-off, and its passengers were moved to another aircraft.

The threat messages claimed to be from fighters of a group calling itself the Male State. One message sent to Sofia airport said explosive devices had been placed on three Ryanair flights heading for Bulgaria, and the same text was sent to the company that runs Rome's airports, aimed at two flights heading for Rome. A British airport also received a similar hoax threat. There is a far-right movement in Russia called the Male State, but the news agency AFP said it could not confirm any link to the messages.

The threats were not new this week. On Monday, a Wizz Air flight from London's Luton airport to Sofia was diverted to Budapest, and on Tuesday, hoax threats targeted up to 10 flights and a cruise ship, closing an airport in Warsaw. The flights hit on Thursday were a tiny share of the 25,072 flights scheduled across Europe that day, and most of them landed at their original airports. The European Union's transport commissioner said it was too early to say whether the incidents were linked.
