Herzog: no clear winner to form Israel government

Israel's President Isaac Herzog has said it is not clear who can form the country's next government, after two days of talks in which 67 lawmakers backed Gadi Eisenkot and 46 backed Benjamin Netanyahu.

6 November 2026 | World | By WPS Staff

Sources: Al Jazeera, ABP Live (PTI)

Israel's President Isaac Herzog has said it is not clear who can form the country's next government, after two days of talks with the parties elected to the Knesset. Speaking on Thursday evening, he said he had met representatives of all the factions, and that 67 members of the Knesset had recommended Gadi Eisenkot to form a government, while 46 had recommended Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

The talks followed the general election on 27 October, in which no side won the 61 seats needed for a majority in the 120-seat Knesset. Netanyahu's bloc won 53 seats, Eisenkot's bloc won 48, and the Arab-led parties won a record 19. The Central Elections Committee certified the results on 4 November after checking the records of 9,069 polling stations, and said it had found no evidence of irregularities that changed the outcome.

Netanyahu and his allies had claimed, without evidence, that the Arab-led parties had stolen the election, possibly with help from abroad. The committee rejected those claims. The head of the committee, Supreme Court Justice Noam Sohlberg, said those casting doubt on the results posed "a real danger" to Israel's democracy, and that the results, as lawfully determined, were binding on all.

Eisenkot's support grew when Ra'am, one of the Arab-led parties, said it would recommend him, bringing his expected support to 56 lawmakers, still short of a majority. The other Arab-led party, the Joint List, had not announced its position, but was expected to coordinate with Eisenkot. United Torah Judaism, part of Netanyahu's bloc, decided not to recommend the prime minister, which brought his recommendations down to 46. Herzog said the picture looked clear from the recommendations, but the mutual vetoes between the blocs and inside them meant it was not clear who could actually build a government.

Herzog said he would task a member of the Knesset with forming a government on Sunday, and that he would first hold personal talks with some party leaders to try to build a stable government. The chosen candidate would then have 28 days to form a government, with a possible 14-day extension. If neither man succeeds, Israel would hold another election early next year. Herzog urged both sides to compromise to avoid a new election, saying "not every commitment and promise can be fulfilled."
