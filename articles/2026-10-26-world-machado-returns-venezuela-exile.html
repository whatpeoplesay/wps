Machado returns to Venezuela after nearly a year away

Nobel Peace Prize winner Maria Corina Machado returned to Venezuela on Sunday after nearly a year outside the country, appearing before jubilant crowds at a church in Santa Rosa.

26 October 2026 | World | By WPS Staff

Sources: Reuters, eNCA

Venezuelan opposition leader Maria Corina Machado returned to Venezuela on Sunday after nearly a year outside the country. She announced her arrival on social media early on Sunday, writing: "I'm here. With you all." Her press team shared images of her in central-western Lara state, in the Plaza del Pueblo de Santa Rosa, where she appeared at a church.

Machado, who won the Nobel Peace Prize, had been barred by the government of former president Nicolas Maduro from leaving Venezuela, but she secretly left in late 2025 to receive the prize, after spending more than a year living mostly in hiding. Maduro was captured in a United States military operation in January and jailed in the United States, and his deputy, Delcy Rodriguez, took over as interim president with Washington's blessing. The interim government has promised elections, but so far it has not set a date.

Her return has strained her relationship with the administration of United States President Donald Trump, which she had sought to cultivate as an ally since before Maduro was removed from power. Trump has repeatedly expressed support for Rodriguez and hailed deals on oil production and other business ventures by American companies in Venezuela, and in September he said it was too soon to consider democratic elections. Speaking at the White House on Sunday, Trump said he did not know anything about Machado's return, calling her "a very nice woman" but adding: "But we're doing really well in Venezuela." In a Fox News interview on Sunday, he also said the United States had "total control of Venezuela" and was taking out millions of barrels of oil a week.

United States Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the conditions allowing Machado's safe return were the direct result of United States supported reconciliation talks, and he urged her to support those talks going forward. He said the talks represent the best chance Venezuela has had in thirty years to peacefully achieve not just a free and fair election but an enduring democracy. A faction of the opposition and the interim government held their latest session of talks this month, aiming to remake the top court and electoral council ahead of eventual elections, but those talks do not include Machado's political movement.

Machado plans to meet victims of the deadly earthquakes that struck Venezuela in June, as well as the families of political prisoners, the international community and leaders from the democratic movement in Venezuela, her top foreign affairs adviser, David Smolansky, told Reuters. Smolansky said she will work proactively for a date for free and fair elections. Edmundo Gonzalez, a former presidential candidate for Machado's movement who has been living in Spain, confirmed her return in his own post and said he would soon join her. Machado has said only a serious, democratic government can provide the stability needed by investors, and in June she said she would do whatever it took to come home and help the survivors of the twin earthquakes that month, which killed at least 6,500 people and left thousands missing.
