Learjet may have broken up before Florida crash that killed nine, investigators say

US investigators say a Learjet that crashed in Florida, killing all nine people on board, may have broken up before impact, with weather a major focus of the probe.

3 November 2026 | US | By WPS Staff

Sources: AFP, AP, Reuters

A private Learjet flying from Austin, Texas, to the Bahamas crashed in a residential area in Palm Beach County, Florida, on Sunday, killing all nine people on board.

The seven passengers were tech entrepreneur Adam Robinson, his wife Helen Sharp, their children Emma, 4, and Charlie, 2, their nanny Anita Maria Cetina, and friends Charmi Fehr and Rachael Reed. The co-pilot was 20-year-old Nathalia Gadelha Filgueiras, who had earned her pilot license at 17. The identity of the other pilot was not immediately disclosed.

At a briefing on Monday, National Transportation Safety Board member Tom Chapman said weather was a major focus of the investigation. Cockpit audio recorded the crew reporting moderate turbulence and ice, saying they were managing the ice and picking up precipitation, before a mayday call reporting a descent of 10,000 feet per minute.

Chapman said investigators had some evidence that the aircraft broke up before impact and that parts could be found some distance from the impact site. He also said it was not yet known who was flying the plane when it ran into trouble, and that maintenance records showed a cockpit recorder had previously been fitted, but investigators did not know if it was on board or could be recovered.

The Learjet 60, operated by Aurora Aviation as Upper Jets, had left Austin at 1537 GMT on Sunday for North Eleuthera in the Bahamas. Heavy rain and thunderstorms were reported in the area where it went down, and the investigation is continuing.
