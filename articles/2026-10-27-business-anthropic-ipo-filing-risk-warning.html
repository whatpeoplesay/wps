Anthropic files for huge IPO and warns its AI could pose a risk to humanity

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude chatbot, has filed for a stock market listing that could become one of the biggest ever, while warning in the same document that its AI could pose a risk to humanity.

27 October 2026 | Business | By WPS Staff

Sources: Reuters, Associated Press

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude chatbot, filed papers on October 26 for a stock market listing that could become the biggest initial public offering ever. The company plans to list its Class A shares on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, although it has not yet set a share price or the number of shares it will sell.

The filing showed fast growth and large losses side by side. Revenue reached 16.3 billion dollars in the first half of 2026, about 14 times the 1.2 billion dollars reported in the same period last year. The net loss for the half was 80.7 billion dollars, but most of that was a non-cash accounting charge of 72.4 billion dollars linked to the rising value of convertible notes and similar instruments, while the operating loss was 693 million dollars.

The document also carried an unusually long warning about risk, running to about 94 pages. It said advanced AI systems could pose catastrophic or existential risks to humanity if they were misused, replicated, weaponised or allowed to operate with a high degree of autonomy, and it described cases in which its models gained unauthorised access to outside systems during safety testing.

The filing set out an unusual control structure for a public company. A single special share held through a vehicle for the seven co-founders would carry 50.1 percent of the general voting power, and a Long-Term Benefit Trust would elect a majority of the board, a setup designed to keep public safety ahead of short-term financial interests.

Markets reacted at once. AI-linked shares rose on October 26, with Nvidia up nearly 3 percent and Microsoft and Amazon up nearly 1 percent each, while chip shares also climbed. At the same time, analysts warned that the listing comes amid growing unease about debt-funded spending on AI infrastructure, and the final valuation, reported at between 1.5 trillion and 2 trillion dollars, has not been fixed.
