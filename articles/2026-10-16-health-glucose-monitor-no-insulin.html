Glucose monitors help type 2 diabetes patients who do not use insulin, trial finds

A trial found that wearable glucose monitors improved blood sugar control in adults with type 2 diabetes who do not use insulin, a group for whom the devices were not previously recommended.

16 October 2026 | Health | By WPS Staff

Sources: Reuters, The New England Journal of Medicine

Wearable glucose monitoring devices also help patients with diabetes who do not use insulin, a new study found. Until now, the devices had been recommended only for patients receiving insulin.

Read More

In a six-month trial sponsored by device maker Dexcom, 283 adults with type 2 diabetes who were not using insulin received either continuous glucose monitoring with the Dexcom G7, a coin-size disc worn on the upper arm, or routine care. The results were reported in The New England Journal of Medicine.

The average hemoglobin A1c, a measure of average blood sugar levels over time, fell from 8.8 percent to 7.2 percent in the monitoring group, and from 8.7 percent to 8.0 percent in the routine-care group. After accounting for individual risk factors, Dexcom G7 users had a nearly 1 percent greater reduction in A1c than patients getting routine care.

People using the wearable device also spent about five extra hours per day in the glucose target range of 70 to 180 mg/dL compared with those receiving routine care. The benefit was seen regardless of age, sex, race and ethnicity, body mass index, income, education, health insurance, and use of glucose-lowering medicines.

When the study ended, 97 percent of participants were still using the monitors, most on a daily basis. Study leader Dr. Roy Beck of the Jaeb Center for Health Research in Tampa, Florida, said the steady benefit across a broad range of people and treatment plans supports considering a continuous glucose monitor much earlier in type 2 diabetes care, before insulin is needed.
