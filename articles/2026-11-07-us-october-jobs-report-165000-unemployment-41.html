US adds 165,000 jobs in October, unemployment falls to 4.1%

US employers added 165,000 jobs in October, far more than economists expected, and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said on Friday.

7 November 2026 | US | By WPS Staff

Sources: Reuters, IndustryWeek

The US economy added 165,000 jobs in October, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said on Friday, November 6. The number was far above the 65,000 jobs economists polled by Reuters had expected. The unemployment rate fell to 4.1 percent, down from 4.2 percent in September.

September's job gain was revised up, from 29,000 to 60,000, and August and September together were revised up by 84,000. Hiring has now averaged about 137,000 jobs a month over the past three months, a much healthier pace than the market had expected.

Most of the new jobs came in professional and business services, which added 70,000 jobs, and health care, which added 42,000. Transportation and warehousing added about 25,000 jobs, while leisure and hospitality lost 13,000 jobs. Average hourly earnings rose 0.4 percent in the month to 38.00 dollars, up 3.1 percent from a year earlier.

There was a softer side to the report too. The number of people working part time because they could not find full-time work rose by 339,000 to 4.8 million. About 7.1 million people were unemployed in October. The unemployment rate has stayed in a narrow range of 4.1 to 4.3 percent since March.

Markets reacted quickly. Stocks were mixed after the report, Treasury yields rose, and bets that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates in December rose to 78 percent from 70 percent. Analysts said the strong report gives the Fed more room to focus on inflation, which is being pushed up by the conflict in the Middle East. The next jobs report, for November, is due on December 4.
