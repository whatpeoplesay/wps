Trump to ban AI term in relevant documents, pushes super intelligence

Donald Trump said on October 27 he will ban the terms artificial intelligence and AI in relevant documents, replacing them with super intelligence and SI, in the latest step in a renaming push that began with a September executive order.

28 October 2026 | Technology | By WPS Staff

Sources: Reuters, ANI

US President Donald Trump said on October 27 that he will soon ban the terms artificial intelligence and AI in relevant documents, and replace them with super intelligence and SI. He made the announcement in a post on Truth Social, where he also asked why The Wall Street Journal was still using the old term.

Read More

In the post, Trump called artificial intelligence a very bad and inaccurate name. He said super intelligence was the name that virtually everyone else was using, or soon would be. He said the ban would come in short order and would cover all relevant documents and everything else, because of the inaccuracy of the old term.

The announcement follows an executive order signed on September 30 titled Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence. That order directed federal executive departments and agencies to use super intelligence and SI instead of artificial intelligence and AI in official communications and operations, to the maximum extent permitted by law.

Under the order, the new wording is to be used in government correspondence, public communications, official websites, reports and administrative policy documents. Previously issued regulations, existing government contracts, grants and historical archival records are not to be changed under the order.

The order also set a 60-day deadline for the Assistant to the President for Science and Technology to submit proposed legislative language to Congress for an updated federal statutory definition of super intelligence, with matching changes to existing laws. The move came after Trump hosted technology executives at the White House to discuss advanced systems and their safe development.
