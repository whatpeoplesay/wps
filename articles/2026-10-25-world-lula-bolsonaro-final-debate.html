Lula and Bolsonaro clash in final debate before Brazil runoff

Lula and Flavio Bolsonaro traded sharp lines on prices, crime and spending in their last televised debate, with polls tied two days before Brazil votes.

25 October 2026 | World | By WPS Staff

Sources: The Hindu, Kuwait Times

Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva and right wing challenger Flavio Bolsonaro held their final televised debate on Friday, October 23, two days before the presidential runoff on Sunday, October 25. The debate was calmer and more measured than their first meeting, but neither candidate held back from cutting remarks. Polls put the two men tied ahead of the vote, after Bolsonaro finished first in the first round.

Their first debate five days earlier had been full of insults and almost empty of concrete policy proposals. This time the candidates questioned each other on the economy, infrastructure and regional development, though the exchanges stayed sharp. Lula attacked the 45 year old senator for his lack of experience, while Bolsonaro accused the president of leaving behind a broken country. Lula said his rival did not know what hunger is, and Bolsonaro asked why things are so expensive.

A Datafolha survey published on Thursday, October 22, showed the pair tied at 50 percent each. Two weeks earlier, polls had given Bolsonaro a four point lead. Bolsonaro is a senator and the son of far right former president Jair Bolsonaro. The result could see Latin America's largest country join a regional shift to the right championed by United States President Donald Trump, or stay a left wing stronghold.

Lula, 80, repeatedly urged viewers to Google Bolsonaro's claims, and the senator shot back by asking who governs Brazil, him or Google. Bolsonaro mocked Lula over the finger he lost in a workplace accident as a young metalworker, saying it was no use giving with one hand and taking away with nine fingers. Lula replied that he would not allow a militiaman to call him nine fingers, a reference to allegations that his rival has ties to paramilitary groups in Rio de Janeiro. Bolsonaro apologised, but asked Lula to apologise to Brazilians who suffer from insecurity. Lula also challenged his rival's plan to take a chainsaw to public spending in the style of Argentina's leader Javier Milei, while Bolsonaro promised to tackle crime, create 500,000 more prison places and slash government spending.

Legal disputes have dominated the bitter campaign, and both candidates went to court in the same week to demand that the other be disqualified over social media abuses. Lula's campaign accused tech giant Meta of interference in Bolsonaro's favour through alleged algorithmic manipulation. Meta denied favouring either side, but acknowledged that some of Lula's campaign ads had been mistakenly deactivated because of technical errors. An electoral court gave Meta and X 24 hours on October 22 to explain how they distribute political content and ads and how they use artificial intelligence. Bolsonaro's campaign, in turn, accused Lula's camp on October 23 of using public resources and digital structures to interfere in the election.
