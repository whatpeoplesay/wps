Pilot asks passengers to vote after dog urinates on plane

Waiting passengers were reportedly asked to vote by a show of hands after a pilot said a dog had urinated on the previous flight, in a clip that passed 10 million views and split viewers.

30 October 2026 | Travel | By WPS Staff

Sources: New York Post, Simple Flying

Passengers waiting to board a flight were reportedly given an unusual choice by their pilot. After a dog urinated in the cabin on the previous flight, the pilot said the plane could either be cleaned, which would delay departure, or leave on time with the smell still in the cabin. He then asked the waiting passengers to vote by raising their hands.

The clip was shared on social media by television host Jennifer Coffey, who made clear that it was not her own flight. Reports said the video appeared to show a Delta Air Lines gate at LaGuardia Airport in New York, but that identification has not been independently confirmed, and no flight number or aircraft has been confirmed.

The short clip does not show what the passengers finally chose. Both reports say the outcome of the vote remains unclear, including whether the plane was cleaned before boarding or whether the flight left on time. The video still spread fast, passing 10 million views, and Coffey later said she had no idea it would become so divisive.

Online reaction split in two directions. Some people said they would rather leave on time, pointing out that the dog's accident was not deliberate and that human passengers can also leave a cabin unpleasant. Others said a plane in that state should not fly as it was, and some said they would want a different aircraft or a refund, although it is unclear whether a plane swap was possible.

The wider rules for dogs on planes draw a line between service dogs and pets. In the United States, airlines must recognise service dogs with the right paperwork, while pet dogs face size, weight and age limits and, in the cabin, cannot sit in bulkhead or exit rows. The Port Authority, which runs LaGuardia, referred questions to Delta, and Delta had not immediately responded to the Post's request for comment.
