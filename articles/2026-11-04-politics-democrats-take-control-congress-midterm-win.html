Democrats take control of Congress in sweeping midterm win

Democrats have won control of both the Senate and the House in Tuesday's midterm elections, ending Republican control of Congress and setting up divided government in Washington.

4 November 2026 | Politics | By WPS Staff

Sources: Reuters, CNN

Democrats have taken control of both chambers of the United States Congress in Tuesday's midterm elections, according to projections reported by Reuters and CNN. The results end two years in which Republicans held Congress while Donald Trump was president, and they set up a new period of divided government in Washington.

Democrats flipped seven Republican-held Senate seats, in Iowa, Kansas, Maine, North Carolina, Ohio, South Carolina and Texas, according to CNN. In Kansas, Democrats won a Senate race for the first time since the 1930s, and in South Carolina they won a seat that had been in Republican hands since 1964. In the House, Democrats won all 20 of the most competitive races decided so far, Reuters reported, and a Republican effort to redraw House districts to help the party largely failed.

Reuters reported that voters were driven by deep frustration over the war with Iran, worry about the high cost of living, and anger at Trump's stewardship of the country. Democrats campaigned on corruption and affordability, blaming Trump's tariff policy and the Iran war for driving up costs. Exit polls reported by Reuters found that half of voters saw the election as a chance to rebuke the president, three quarters said they were angry or dissatisfied with the direction of the country, and 70 percent said they wanted Congress to provide more checks and balances on the president.

Trump accepted the outcome, congratulating Democrats in an interview with Fox News, Reuters reported. He said he would work with Democrats on issues such as immigration, while threatening to veto bills he called insane. On social media on Tuesday night, he wrote that he was not on the ballot and that Republicans would have won in a landslide if he had been. He later told Fox News that he did not view the result as a hit on him.

Once seated in January, Democrats are expected to use their new power to investigate Trump's administration, family and allies, and to demand concessions on major legislation, Reuters reported. The Democratic Senate can also block Trump's nominees, including any Supreme Court justice if a vacancy arises. A small number of competitive Senate races, in Mississippi, Michigan and Alaska, had yet to be called early on Wednesday, according to Reuters.
