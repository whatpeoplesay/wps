Marty Erlichman, Barbra Streisand's manager for six decades, dies at 97

Marty Erlichman, who discovered Barbra Streisand when she was 19 and managed her for more than six decades without a contract, has died at 97.

2 November 2026 | Culture | By WPS Staff

Sources: Variety, The Hollywood Reporter

Marty Erlichman, the manager who discovered Barbra Streisand and guided her career for more than six decades, has died at the age of 97. Family representatives announced his death on Sunday, 1 November, saying he died on Saturday, 31 October, of age-related causes.

Read More

Erlichman first saw Streisand when she was 19 and singing as an opening act in a New York nightclub. He stayed as her manager for the rest of his working life, and she never had another manager. The two worked without a signed contract, on trust built over decades.

He handled the business side of her career, including recording, theatre, film, television and live concerts. He produced her 2000 Millennium Concert and her world tour in 2006 and 2007, and he was executive producer of her 1994 television special, Barbra: The Concert, which won him an Emmy Award and a Peabody Award.

His work reached beyond Streisand. His other clients included the folk acts The Clancy Brothers and Tommy Makem, the pop group Gary Puckett and The Union Gap, comedian Chevy Chase, heavyweight champion George Foreman and songwriter Bob Crewe. He also produced films, including Streisand's 1974 comedy For Pete's Sake. He stepped back from daily management in March 2026, when he was 96.

Streisand paid tribute on Instagram, saying she truly thought he would live forever, that he could never be replaced, and that she would always be grateful for his friendship, loyalty and support. His family described him as a giant of Hollywood to the world and a devoted husband, father and grandfather to them. He is survived by his wife, Miko Mayama Erlichman, their son and four grandchildren. Funeral arrangements have not been announced.
