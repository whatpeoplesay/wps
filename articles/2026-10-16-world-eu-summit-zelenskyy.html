EU leaders back Zelenskyy at Brussels summit as US pressure grows

EU leaders met Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Brussels on Friday. He asked for energy, air defence and funding help to get Ukraine through the winter.

16 October 2026 | World | By WPS Staff

Sources: Reuters, Ukrinform via Mezha

European Union leaders met Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Brussels on Friday, as tensions grew between Kyiv and Washington over Ukrainian strikes on Russian oil refineries. The leaders were set to show support for Zelenskyy at a summit that focused on how Ukraine gets through the coming winter.

Zelenskyy said Ukraine wants the war to end as soon as possible, but sees no sign that Russia is ready to end it. He said the talks would cover a winter package of energy, air defence and funding, and Ukraine's needs for the rest of this year and for 2027.

The meeting came days after US President Donald Trump said Ukraine and Russia had agreed not to target each other's energy sites. Ukraine then struck a Russian oil refinery and said it was dropping that pause. Trump called the Ukrainian strikes a disgrace and said they were pushing up world oil prices. EU leaders also criticised a US diesel deal with Russia, and the Trump administration was reported to be weighing looser sanctions on tankers that move Russian oil.

At the same time, Russian strikes kept hitting Ukrainian cities. Drone strikes on Kharkiv killed three people, according to local authorities. Strikes on the Volyn region, which borders Poland, left 370,000 people without power. Emergency power cuts were put in place in Kyiv during the strikes.

Ukraine still faces a large funding gap. Its finance minister has said the country faces an unfunded budget gap of 32.6 billion dollars for next year, not counting an estimated 40 billion dollars in direct military supplies from allies. Dutch Prime Minister Rob Jetten said he was unhappy with the number of countries willing to pledge new financial support. Russia, for its part, moved to place local assets of French, German, Swedish and Dutch companies under temporary management, and President Vladimir Putin played down the effect of the Ukrainian refinery strikes.
