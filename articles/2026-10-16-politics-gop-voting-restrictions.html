GOP-led states have made it harder to vote before the midterms

Voters in at least 22 states face new hurdles this November. Republican-led states and counties have tightened registration, ID, and mail ballot rules since 2022.

16 October 2026 | Politics | By WPS Staff

Sources: CNN, Associated Press

Voters in at least 22 states face new hurdles when they cast ballots this November, compared with the 2024 election, according to a recent Brennan Center report. Republican lawmakers and officials in states and counties have added stricter registration rules, ID rules, and cuts to early voting sites, polling places, and ballot drop boxes. The changes, all passed since the last midterm election in 2022, could affect competitive Senate races in Iowa, Ohio, North Carolina, and Texas, and toss-up House races. Democrats need three seats to retake the House and four to win the Senate.

In Tarrant County, Texas, part of a key swing district, mail-in voters must now provide a Social Security or state ID number. In-person voters will find nearly 100 polling places shuttered, including all sites in one zip code that is mostly Black and Latino. On September 1, the Republican-controlled county commissioners voted 3-2 along party lines to cut polling sites by nearly 30 percent, a change the county judge said was needed to save money. Opponents said the cuts will make it harder to vote for groups seen as likely to back Democrats. A lawsuit seeking to restore the sites was filed on October 2, and a judge this week denied the request.

In Ohio, voters face one of the strictest voter ID laws in the country for the first time in a midterm. The law, passed by the Republican-controlled legislature in 2023, requires a photo ID to vote, so voters can no longer use digital IDs, paychecks, or other documents as identification or to prove residency. It also shortened the deadlines to request absentee ballots and to fix errors on ballots, and it ended early voting on the Monday before Election Day. Each county is limited to one drop box, open only during business hours, and only voters with a documented disability who cannot physically enter a polling place can use curbside voting.

In North Carolina, county election boards voted not to place early-voting sites near or on several college campuses, including North Carolina A&T State University and UNC-Greensboro. Boards also ended Sunday early voting in 23 counties, a day that has mattered to Black churches that organize members to vote after services. The chairman of the State Board of Elections said he opposed campus sites because of limited parking, and opposed Sunday voting because it is the Lord's Day. Students pushed back at board meetings for months, without success. In 2024, the Republican-controlled legislature also moved control of the state board of elections from the governor, a Democrat, to the state auditor, a Republican.

In Iowa, voters who show up without an accepted ID can no longer have another voter vouch for them. They must now use a provisional ballot and return by the Monday after Election Day with a valid ID. In Kansas, lawmakers overrode the governor's veto to pass the SAVE Kansas Act, which expands ways to purge voter rolls and restricts third-party voter registration efforts. Lawmakers also overrode a veto of a law that would require mail-in ballots to arrive by 7 p.m. on Election Day, but a court ruling blocking that law while a lawsuit is heard was left standing. Supporters of the changes say they are needed to make elections more secure. Critics say they could lower turnout by making it harder for first-time voters and for people without driver's licenses, such as some seniors and disabled voters, to cast ballots.
