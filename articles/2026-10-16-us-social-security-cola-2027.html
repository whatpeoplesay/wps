Social Security benefits to rise 3.6 percent in 2027, agency says

The Social Security Administration announced a 3.6 percent cost-of-living adjustment for 2027. The average retirement benefit will rise by about $75 a month.

16 October 2026 | US | By WPS Staff

Sources: Social Security Administration, Reuters

The Social Security Administration announced on Wednesday, October 14, that Social Security benefits and Supplemental Security Income payments will rise 3.6 percent in 2027. The agency said the average Social Security retirement benefit will go up by about $75 a month.

Read More

Nearly 72 million Social Security beneficiaries will see the higher payments starting in January 2027. About 7.3 million people who receive Supplemental Security Income will see the increase in payments dated December 31, 2026. Some people receive both types of benefits, so about 76 million Americans are covered in total.

The yearly adjustment, known as COLA, is set by law and follows the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers, or CPI-W, measured by the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Reuters reported that the CPI-W averaged about 3.6 percent higher across July, August and September than in the same months a year earlier.

People with a personal my Social Security account can view their COLA notice online starting in late November, up to three weeks earlier than the mailed notice. Those who want the online notice must create an account and choose paperless notices by November 18. Everyone else will get the notice by mail in December.

The agency said the new notice is a simplified one-page letter in plain language, with the exact dates and dollar amounts of the new benefit and any deductions. Other 2027 changes include a rise in the maximum earnings subject to Social Security tax, to $192,300 from $184,500. Information about Medicare changes for 2027 will be available at medicare.gov.
