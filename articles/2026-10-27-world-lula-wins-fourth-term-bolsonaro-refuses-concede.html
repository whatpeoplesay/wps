Lula wins fourth term as Bolsonaro refuses to concede

Brazil's electoral court called the runoff for Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva on Sunday night, giving him a fourth term. His rival Flavio Bolsonaro refused to accept the result and said he would seek to have it annulled.

27 October 2026 | World | By WPS Staff

Sources: Reuters, Al Jazeera, Reuters

Brazil's Superior Electoral Court called the presidential runoff for leftist President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva shortly after 9pm Brasilia time on Sunday. Lula earned more than 51 percent of the vote and more than 64 million ballots, while right-wing Senator Flavio Bolsonaro took 48 percent and more than 59 million ballots, a gap of more than 4 million votes.

The win completed a comeback after the first round on October 4, when Lula trailed Bolsonaro with 45 percent against 47 percent, both short of the 50 percent needed to avoid a runoff. No other politician in modern Brazilian history has lost the first round of a presidential election and then won the second. In his victory speech, Lula thanked voters who cast ballots to secure democracy in the country, and said that although he did not choose the Congress, with its rightward tilt, it was with that Congress that he would vote, work and get things approved.

Bolsonaro has so far refused to concede and said he would seek an annulment of the results. He said it was impossible to acknowledge the result and accused the Supreme Court of having changed the rules of the game mid-game, pointing to a decision by Justice Cristiano Zanin to extend voting by two hours. Both campaigns had petitioned the court for an extension, after reports that public transport was suspended in some areas and heavy rains affected southern areas seen as Bolsonaro strongholds, and polls stayed open until 7pm Brasilia time.

United States President Donald Trump weighed in on Monday, telling reporters without citing evidence that the Brazilian election looked like another rigged election, and that elections have to be indisputable and it looked very bad to him. His eldest son, Donald Trump Jr, also suggested judicial malpractice over the extension. Leaders elsewhere welcomed the result, including Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum, who said long live democracy and the people of Brazil had won, and United States Senator Bernie Sanders, who called the re-election extraordinary.

Lula, at age 80, will be sworn in on January 5, 2027, for an unprecedented fourth non-consecutive term, when he will beat his own record as the oldest Brazilian president to take the oath since the country's return to democracy. Questions remain over how Bolsonaro's base and the Trump administration will react to the result, and over whether his legislative priorities can advance in what Al Jazeera describes as the most right-wing Congress in recent memory.
