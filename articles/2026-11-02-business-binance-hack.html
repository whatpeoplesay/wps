FBI blames North Korea for $6.3bn Binance hack as withdrawals resume

Binance has resumed withdrawals in phases after a record $6.3 billion theft, as the FBI blames North Korea-linked hackers and the exchange says it will cover the losses itself.

2 November 2026 | Business | By WPS Staff

Sources: CNN, Channel NewsAsia

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, has begun bringing back withdrawals in stages after about $6.3 billion was stolen from its digital wallets last week. The FBI said on Saturday that North Korea, through a group it calls TraderTraitor, was behind the theft, which has been described as the largest crypto theft on record.

The bulk of the theft happened in about 20 minutes late on Thursday, 29 October, according to blockchain analysis firms TRM Labs and Chainalysis. The firms said the attackers were able to order Binance wallets to empty into wallets controlled by the hackers, in a pattern similar to the $1.5 billion Bybit theft in February 2025, which the FBI also linked to North Korea. The FBI said TraderTraitor actors were converting the stolen assets mainly into Ethereum and were expected to try to move them further through decentralized finance and instant swap services.

Binance stopped withdrawals on Friday after the breach, then said early on Sunday that it was resuming them in phases. Withdrawals for 15 digital currencies, including Bitcoin, Solana and Ethereum, were back by 7:36 a.m. ET, and the company said it had processed about $4.6 billion in withdrawals and $1 billion in deposits by 10:30 a.m. ET. It warned that some users could still face delays or longer processing times while the remaining systems are tested.

Binance said about 95 percent of the stolen amount is now covered and that it will fully absorb the loss rather than pass it to users. The money came from its Secure Asset Fund for Users, a reserve of roughly $1 billion in Bitcoin, together with its corporate treasury and its Futures insurance fund. The remaining 5 percent is in less liquid tokens that take longer to source, while the top 10 digital currencies are fully backed. Industry partners have also frozen about $3 billion of the stolen assets.

Binance said it has carried out security reviews, worked with law enforcement and partners to trace and recover funds, and thanked the FBI for its support. North Korea has routinely denied involvement in such cyber thefts, and its missions did not immediately respond to requests for comment. The company said it will share details of the next phase of withdrawals as soon as it can, and that market conditions are stable.
