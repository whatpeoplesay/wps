France imposes overnight curfews after weekend of riots and looting

PM Sébastien Lecornu says targeted curfews kept the peace overnight; more curfews to be decided Tuesday. Over 2,500 people have been arrested since the unrest began.

20 October 2026 | World | By WPS Staff

Sources: The Straits Times (Reuters), NTV World

France has imposed overnight curfews in parts of Paris and several other cities after a weekend of riots and looting tied to protests over living costs, school conditions and public services. The decision followed a crisis meeting on Monday, where Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu gave local mayors the power to set curfews wherever they are needed.

In Paris, police set curfews from 11 pm to 6 am around Place de la République, where protesters had gathered over the weekend, and the Les Halles shopping district. Unauthorised gatherings were also banned in other parts of the city centre. Partial curfews and limits on gatherings were announced in Marseille, Bordeaux, Nice and Lyon in the south, and Lille and other towns in the north.

On Tuesday, Lecornu said the targeted curfews had helped keep the peace overnight, thanking police officers and gendarmes in a post on X. He said more curfews would be decided locally on Tuesday evening wherever the situation requires it. He did not rule out declaring a state of emergency, and promised a law to make those who damaged property pay, along with a partial unemployment fund for people who cannot work because of the riots.

Officials say more than 2,500 people have been arrested since the unrest began, while about 1,000 police officers and gendarmes were injured. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez admitted that police had “lost control” in some cities; he offered to resign on Sunday, but President Emmanuel Macron rejected it. Justice Minister Gérald Darmanin said 123 people were in detention awaiting trial while about 2,500 remained in custody for questioning. Two hundred investigators are reviewing CCTV footage to find suspects, and business groups say more than 2,000 shops were damaged.

The protests began last month with students demanding better school conditions, then grew into a national movement over rising prices and underfunded public services, drawing in trade unions and other protest groups. France’s main student union UNEF pledged to keep up the pressure, and protest groups have announced a new round of demonstrations for October 24, with yellow vest groups calling for scattered actions over fuel costs.
