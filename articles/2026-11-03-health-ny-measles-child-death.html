First New York Measles Death in a Child in Over 30 Years

A child in Lewis County, New York, has died after contracting measles, the state's first such death in more than 30 years, as health officials urged residents to stay up to date on vaccination.

3 November 2026 | Health | By WPS Staff

Sources: CNN, Reuters

A child in Lewis County, New York, has died after contracting measles, state and county health officials announced on Monday. It is the first measles related death in New York in more than 30 years.

Read More

The New York State Department of Health and Lewis County Public Health said in a joint statement that they were deeply saddened by the death. They offered condolences to the child's family and loved ones, asked that their privacy be respected, and urged New Yorkers to stay up to date on measles, mumps and rubella vaccinations.

Governor Kathy Hochul said she was heartbroken by the news. She said that as a mother and grandmother she could not imagine what the family was going through, and that vaccination remained the best protection against measles. She called the death a devastating reminder of what was at stake when progress against preventable illness was weakened.

The death came during an outbreak that led Hochul to declare a statewide disaster emergency last month. As of October 24, New York had reported 178 measles cases this year, with 170 outside New York City and eight in the city. Lewis County had reported 15 cases as of October 14, all in people who had not received the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine. At the state's request, federal health officials deployed epidemiologists and other experts to work with state officials on surveillance, planning and outbreak response.

Across the United States, more than 4,800 measles cases have been reported this year, the most since 1991. Health officials say about one in five people who are not vaccinated and catch measles need hospital care, and two doses of the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine provide about 97 percent protection against infection.
