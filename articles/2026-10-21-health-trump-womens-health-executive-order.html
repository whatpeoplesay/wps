Trump Signs Executive Order Creating NIH Women's Health Division

President Trump signed an order creating a new NIH Women's Health Division and a White House task force. Online, the reaction splits between praise for the move and doubt about the timing.

21 October 2026 | Health | By WPS Staff

Sources: HHS.gov, USA Today

President Donald Trump signed an executive order on Monday, October 19, 2026, creating a new Women's Health Division at the National Institutes of Health. The order, titled "Advancing Women's Health to Improve Outcomes in All Phases of Life," was signed at a Rose Garden ceremony at the White House, with First Lady Melania Trump signing alongside him.

Read More

The order also creates the first White House Women's Health Task Force. The task force will be co-chaired by Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. and White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles. It held its first meeting on Monday afternoon. The new NIH division must be set up within 90 days.

According to the White House and the Department of Health and Human Services, the division will push research into conditions that are understudied in women, including heart disease, cancer, autoimmune conditions, chronic pain, endometriosis, fibroids, infertility, maternal health, menopause, Alzheimer's and osteoporosis. The order also asks that sex be treated as a biological variable in research, and that discoveries move faster into patient care. The White House did not give a dollar figure for new funding.

Melania Trump has pressed for the move for months, holding working sessions with doctors in the East Wing, according to USA Today. Speaking at the ceremony, she called it "a transformative moment for women in America" and said, "Beginning today, we will change the reality of women's health." Kennedy said that for generations, women have faced unanswered questions about conditions that affect them uniquely or more than men.

The order does not replace the NIH Office of Research on Women's Health, which has existed since 1990. Former President Joe Biden signed a women's health executive order in March 2024. Critics, including NYU researcher Jennifer Weiss-Wolf, said the new order sits uneasily beside the administration's record on abortion rights and cuts to health research. The White House says the division builds on earlier steps, including the removal of a boxed warning on hormone replacement therapy and the approval of two menopause drugs.
