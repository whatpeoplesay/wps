Akasa Air extends Saudi flight suspension to late November

Akasa Air has pushed its Jeddah suspension to 18 November and its Riyadh suspension to 30 November, as Indian airlines keep Saudi services paused amid the security situation.

2 November 2026 | Travel | By WPS Staff

Sources: The Hindu, Gulf News

Akasa Air said on 1 November that it had extended the suspension of its flights to and from Saudi Arabia. Flights to and from Jeddah will remain suspended until and including 18 November, while flights to and from Riyadh will remain suspended until and including 30 November. The airline said it had assessed the security situation in Saudi Arabia, that passenger and crew safety remained its highest priority, and that it was monitoring developments in real time.

The move came two days after Air India and Air India Express said their flights to and from Jeddah, Riyadh and Dammam would remain suspended until 14 November. IndiGo has also cancelled flights to and from Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam and Madinah until the same date, citing ongoing airspace restrictions in the region. The disruptions have continued amid attacks on civilian infrastructure in Saudi Arabia, including airports, and Yemen's Houthi rebels have claimed responsibility for several attacks on Saudi targets, including airports and energy facilities.

Akasa Air passengers affected by the suspension can choose a full refund, credited to the original payment method within seven days, or reschedule their travel at no extra cost. Passengers who booked through travel agents or other travel partners should contact them directly for help. Air India passengers with bookings issued on or before 30 October for travel up to 14 November can also choose a one-time complimentary rebooking, subject to availability, for travel up to 29 November, or ask for a full refund on cancellation.

Not all routes between India and Saudi Arabia have stopped. Saudi carriers continue to operate some flights to India, and Saudi carrier flynas has announced extra flights from Dammam to Lucknow, Mumbai, Delhi, Hyderabad, Kozhikode and Kochi, and from Riyadh to Delhi, Hyderabad and Kozhikode, on selected dates. The Consulate General of India in Jeddah is coordinating with airlines, local authorities and community organisations to help affected Indian nationals, particularly Umrah pilgrims, and its control rooms in Jeddah and Makkah remain open around the clock.

Passengers have been advised to check flight schedules with their airline before travelling. The Indian Embassy in Riyadh has asked Indian nationals to remain calm and vigilant, to stay indoors in the event of an aerial attack, to follow instructions from local authorities, and not to circulate or repost videos or news about the ongoing developments. Saudi Arabia has a large Indian expatriate population and is also a major destination for Indian pilgrims travelling for Umrah.
