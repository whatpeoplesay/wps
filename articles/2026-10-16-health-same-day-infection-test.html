Same-day test finds hidden infections in critically ill children, study finds

A same-day test that reads all the genetic material in one sample found hidden and drug-resistant infections in critically ill children at Great Ormond Street Hospital in London.

16 October 2026 | Health | By WPS Staff

Sources: BBC News, The Lancet Microbe

A same-day test has been used to find hidden and drug-resistant infections in children in intensive care at Great Ormond Street Hospital in London. The findings, from a study of the hospital's paediatric intensive care unit, have been published in The Lancet Microbe.

Read More

The method is called metagenomic testing. It reads all the genetic material in a single sample, so doctors do not have to decide in advance which germ to look for. The study used breathing samples from 122 critically ill children treated during 2024 and 2025, and the test could look for bacteria, viruses and fungi at the same time.

The test picked up about nine in ten bacteria and viruses in the samples, and all of the fungi. It also found 50 organisms that routine testing had not identified. In about a quarter of cases, the results changed prescribing decisions, including starting, stopping or changing an antibiotic.

One child in the study was 10-year-old Willow Crowther from Kent. She became seriously ill with pneumonia, which led to a seizure and several cardiac arrests, and her family was told she had about an even chance of surviving. The test found a drug-resistant bacterial infection that needed a different antibiotic from the one already prescribed, and after several months in hospital she recovered.

Doctors on the study said older testing required an educated guess about which infection to look for, and that stopping antibiotics that will not work matters in the fight against growing antimicrobial resistance. The testing is being rolled out to around 30 intensive care units across the UK treating adults and children, and the team said it could also give early warning of new infections. The hospital project builds on earlier trials at Guy's and St Thomas' NHS Trust, which first used the method to check for breathing infections in adult patients.
