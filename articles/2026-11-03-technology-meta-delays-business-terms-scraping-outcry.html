Meta delays new business terms after outcry over scraping fears

Meta has put its new business terms on hold after media companies and open-web advocates warned the rules could let it take vast amounts of online content and use it to build competing services.

3 November 2026 | Technology | By WPS Staff

Sources: Associated Press via TechXplore, Moneycontrol

Meta has postponed the rollout of new terms for its business accounts after an outcry from media companies and open-web advocates. The critics said the updated rules would allow the tech giant to scrape vast amounts of data and potentially build services that compete with news publishers.

Read More

The new rules were originally scheduled to go into effect on October 30. They would authorise Meta to use automated software, such as web scrapers and bots, to index, cache, analyse, collect, retrieve, or crawl the domains, web pages, or URLs linked to a business account. The terms would have applied to all business clients, regardless of opposition, and without payment for the extracted content. Critics said Meta would have been able to use the content to build products such as AI services that could compete with news publishers. The new terms only apply to business accounts. Regular users of Meta's platforms, including Instagram and Facebook, can already have their public posts scraped to train AI models.

Opposition to the terms has grown across Europe. On October 28, a court in Norway granted a temporary injunction suspending Meta's new terms at the request of 180 Norwegian media companies, pending a full assessment of their legality. Their case was supported by the Norwegian Media Businesses' Association, whose member newspapers account for approximately 97 percent of Norwegian newspaper circulation. The UK Competition and Markets Authority said on October 30 it was starting its own investigation into the conditions, saying it was concerned that requiring news publishers and other content businesses to accept the terms could amount to an unlawful imposition of unfair trading conditions.

Meta, however, says the terms do not change how the company uses web crawlers. Meta spokesman Andy Stone wrote on X on October 16 that the update is designed to make sure Meta can pull accurate information about products for sale on third-party websites when making on-platform ad and content recommendations, not about scraping news sites. His comments came after a report by the Nieman Journalism Lab at Harvard University, which said that as written, the new terms could be used to argue that Meta can scrape content from news sites and use that content to train its AI products. Nieman Lab said it consulted three legal experts who reviewed the terms at its request. At some point after that report, Meta removed the word train from the updated terms. Meta said on October 29 it was listening to feedback and that for now its existing terms remain in place, adding that it does not plan to make policy changes without a full consultation with industry groups and leaders.

Norwegian media said the postponement was not enough. The Norwegian Media Businesses' Association said it wants Meta to commit in its new terms to respecting publishers' reservations against scraping, to use journalism in AI products only after a separate, voluntary agreement and with payment, and to not marginalise publishers who do not sign the new terms on Meta's social platforms. The Movement for an Open Web, the group which originally complained to the UK regulator, called Meta's move and the regulator's investigation a significant win, but warned it was only the start. The European Commission confirmed it had discussed the proposed new terms with Meta and welcomed the postponement of their entry into force.
