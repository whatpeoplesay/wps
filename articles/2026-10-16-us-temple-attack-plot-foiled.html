US teen plotted mass shooting at Hindu temple in Aurora, FBI says plot foiled

A 19-year-old in suburban Chicago was arrested before a planned mass shooting at a Hindu temple in Aurora. Police say he had been planning the attack for over two years.

16 October 2026 | US | By WPS Staff

Source: NDTV

A teenager in suburban Chicago was arrested before carrying out a mass shooting plot at a Hindu temple last weekend, according to court documents. Prosecutors said the suspect, Alexandros Paporakis, 19, had been planning the attack for over two years and had told his co-workers at a Taco Bell in Palatine that he wanted to kill Indian people.

Police officials in Palatine, Illinois, and FBI investigators said they believe he was planning to attack a large Hindu temple in Aurora, about 40 miles west of Chicago. Police said a red car registered to Paporakis entered a temple parking lot on Sunday around 12:25 p.m. and left less than 15 minutes later. The temple later informed its members of a security threat.

The plan was stopped after a co-worker reported him to police. The Mount Prospect and Palatine police departments located and arrested Paporakis while he was leaving his home on Sunday afternoon, the same day prosecutors believe he planned to carry out the attack. He told a co-worker that his gun had jammed at the scene and that he was fleeing, and sent a video showing how his AR-style rifle had failed to fire.

During searches at his home, police found a rifle, multiple loaded rifle magazines and hundreds of rounds of ammunition, a tactical vest with body armour, and a patch with imagery linked to white supremacist extremism, officials said. They also found items with Nazi symbols and a document described as a manifesto that used dehumanising language about Indian, Jewish and African American people and outlined a plot to kill as many of them as possible.

Paporakis faces felony charges of making a terrorist threat and unlawfully possessing a rifle. Court documents said he described himself as an ordinary white kid going to college and working, while the document described him as a white supremacist filled with hatred. His arrest added to concerns about a rise in physical and verbal attacks against South Asians, especially Indians, in the United States.
