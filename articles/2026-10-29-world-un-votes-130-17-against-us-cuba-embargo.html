UN General Assembly votes 130-17 against US Cuba embargo

The UN General Assembly voted 130-17 on 28 October to call for an end to the US embargo on Cuba, with 25 abstentions, the weakest backing for the yearly call in two decades.

29 October 2026 | World | By WPS Staff

Sources: UN News, Associated Press

The United Nations General Assembly voted on Wednesday, 28 October, to call for an end to the United States embargo on Cuba. The call passed by 130 votes in favour to 17 against, with 25 countries abstaining. It was the 34th time the assembly has made the call.

The backing was the weakest for the resolution in two decades. Last year it passed by 165 votes to seven, with 12 abstentions. The United States had pushed other countries to oppose the resolution or sit out the vote, and Western countries including Germany, Italy and Latvia abstained for the first time in 34 years.

United States Ambassador Mike Waltz rejected the idea of a blockade. He pointed to more than 876 million dollars in United States exports to Cuba in the first eight months of 2026, as well as shipments from Canada, Spain, Mexico, China, Russia, the European Union and United Nations agencies. He said the wealth of the country had not vanished but had changed hands from ordinary Cubans to a ruling elite, and that a vote against the resolution was the only thing standing between what he called a communist kleptocratic regime and another propagandistic victory at the expense of the Cuban people.

Cuban Foreign Minister Bruno Rodriguez Parrilla answered with a running counter showing what he said the embargo had cost since the debate opened on Tuesday, more than 22.9 million dollars. He blamed the embargo for strangling the health system of Cuba, said infant mortality had doubled and tens of thousands of surgeries had been delayed or cancelled, and said cancer medicines could not get in while Cuban-made treatments for Alzheimer disease and dementia could not get out. He said what Cuba demands is to be left in peace, and that Cuba is not a threat, the blockade is.

The resolution is not legally binding, but it reflects world opinion. The embargo was first imposed in 1960, after Fidel Castro led a revolution and nationalised properties belonging to United States citizens and companies. Cuba is now facing a severe energy crisis, and the United States warning in late January of tariffs on any country that sells or provides oil to Cuba has effectively cut off fuel to the island, leading to power outages that affect cooking, water supply and internet and telephone services.
