Police called after 3-year-old kicked out of soccer game

A youth soccer game in Winter Park, Florida, ended with police being called after a 3-year-old player was removed from his league. Video of the boy dribbling past older players has gone viral, and the dispute has drawn wide support online.

27 October 2026 | US | By WPS Staff

Sources: USA Today, PEOPLE

Police were called to a youth soccer game at Martin Luther King Jr. Park in Winter Park, Florida, on Saturday, October 24, after a dispute between league officials and the parents of 3-year-old Nevada Pierre. Videos shared by his family show the boy, who plays in an i9 Sports league against children around five years old, dribbling past older opponents and scoring goals. After officers arrived, the league asked the family to leave and offered a refund for the cost of taking part.

His mother, Martasia Griffin, told officers that other parents were unhappy because their children could not take the ball from her son. She said the family was being pushed out because he played too well, telling police: "They are mad at my son because he actually plays soccer." In a post on Monday, October 26, she shared video of a full game and asked where any pushing by her son could be seen, pushing back against the claim that he had pushed other players.

The league official gave officers a different account. In body camera footage, he said the boy was very advanced for the group and scored a lot of goals, but also pushed other children, and that he had asked referees to remind the boy not to push. He said he had rotated children in earlier games so everyone got a chance to play, that the parents objected to those decisions, and that during the latest game the parents argued with other parents. He told officers he wanted the parents out immediately and would get their names to issue a refund.

Winter Park police said the incident was a civil matter between the parties, not a criminal one. No one was arrested, trespassed, or forced to leave the park, police said, and the family was cooperative with officers. The family told officers the game was over and they were leaving. The league told WESH, as reported by PEOPLE, that it was aware of the concerns, took them very seriously, had reached out to the family, and had begun a review that included speaking with those involved.

The story has since drawn support from well known sports and entertainment figures. Childrens entertainer Ms. Rachel said she was livid, invited the boy to appear on her show, and called on sports figures to show him love and experiences. United States mens national team defender Alex Freeman wrote that what happened was not right and offered to set up gear and a meeting for the boy. Former NFL player Robert Griffin III wrote that punishing a 3-year-old for being too good at a sport was ridiculous.
