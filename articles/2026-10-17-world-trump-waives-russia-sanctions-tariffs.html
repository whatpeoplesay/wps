Trump waives Russia sanctions and tariffs days before they were to take effect

President Donald Trump signed an executive order on Friday waiving many Russia-related sanctions, restrictions and duties days before they were to take effect, telling Congress the waiver is in the national interest of the United States.

17 October 2026 | World | By WPS Staff

Sources: Reuters, Indian Express

President Donald Trump signed an executive order on Friday that waives many sanctions, restrictions and duties that were days away from taking effect under a law he signed last month. In the order released by the White House, Trump said he had sent Congress a certification that issuing the waiver at this time is in the national interests of the United States. The waiver stays in effect until Trump or his top officials decide that further action is appropriate.

The law is the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, named after the late Senator Lindsey Graham, who was its main backer in Congress. It passed with large bipartisan majorities in the House and Senate and was signed by Trump in mid-September. It targeted Russia's energy and defence industries and its shadow fleet of tankers, aiming to cut the money flowing to Moscow for the war in Ukraine.

The law would have allowed tariffs of up to 100 percent within 30 days on all goods brought into the United States from the five largest importers of Russian crude oil or gas. It would also have applied to any country that knowingly bought Russian oil or gas 30 days after the law was enacted, or that ranked among the top five countries helping Russia evade sanctions. Countries such as India, which is among the biggest buyers of Russian oil, had raised concerns about the bill.

The waiver followed a deal Trump said he reached with Russian President Vladimir Putin on October 9, under which Russia would supply diesel to help bring down global energy prices. Trump said Russia would first provide more than 300,000 tonnes, followed by further shipments in November and later months. High fuel prices have been a political problem for Trump ahead of November's midterm elections, in which his Republican Party is fighting to keep its narrow majorities in Congress.

The move came as Trump has warmed toward Russia while publicly criticizing Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, including suggesting that Ukraine needs a new leader. Zelenskyy called the waiver a weak decision. The waiver removes the immediate threat of extra tariffs for major buyers of Russian energy, including India and China. India imports over 88 percent of its crude oil needs, and Russian oil made up 45 percent of its total imports in August at 2.08 million barrels per day.
