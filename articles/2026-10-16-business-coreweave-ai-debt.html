Cost to insure CoreWeave debt jumps past 1,000 points as AI debt worries grow

The cost of insuring CoreWeave debt against default jumped to a record this week. Credit investors are now pricing in a much higher risk that the AI cloud company fails to repay its debt.

16 October 2026 | Business | By WPS Staff

Sources: ABC Money, Reuters

CoreWeave's credit default swaps jumped to a record level this week. A credit default swap is a contract that insures a bondholder if a company fails to repay its debt. The five-year swap spread stood at about 1,038 basis points by midday on October 15, up 164 percent from a year earlier. One basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point.

Different pricing sources put the intraday peak even higher. Bloomberg figures put the peak at 1,194.5 basis points, which it reported as an implied chance of default of more than 60 percent within five years. CNBC showed spreads briefly rising above 1,140 basis points before settling near 1,038, still a 16 percent jump in a single week. Prices differ because these contracts trade over the counter, not on one exchange.

The worry is not demand, it is the balance sheet. Second-quarter 2026 revenue came in at $2.575 billion, more than double the prior year. That same quarter produced a net loss of $626 million. As of June 30, 2026, the company carried about $35.1 billion in debt plus $16.5 billion in lease liabilities, a combined obligation of about $51.6 billion. S&P Global Ratings has CoreWeave at B+, in junk-bond territory. The yield on its 9.75 percent bond due 2031 has climbed to between 14.9 percent and 15.78 percent.

CoreWeave is not the only company under the microscope. Oracle's five-year credit default swap spread also hit a record, near 288 basis points, and SoftBank's climbed to 424 basis points in the same week. That points to credit markets reassessing AI-linked debt broadly, not singling out one balance sheet.

CoreWeave's own camp is not conceding the point. Chief executive Mike Intrator told staff the sell-off was irrational, arguing the company's roughly $104 billion contract backlog comfortably covers its obligations. Deutsche Bank kept a buy rating, telling clients two-thirds of that backlog sits with investment-grade counterparties including Microsoft, Meta, IBM and Caterpillar. In the share market, CoreWeave shares closed at $68.03 on October 15, up 6.27 percent on the day but still down 25.86 percent over the preceding 20 trading days.
