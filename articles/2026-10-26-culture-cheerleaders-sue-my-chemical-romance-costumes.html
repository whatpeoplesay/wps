Cheerleaders sue My Chemical Romance over stage costumes

The company behind the Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders has sued My Chemical Romance over dancers who wore look-alike cheerleader uniforms during the band's Hollywood Bowl show, saying the costumes misled fans and harmed its brand.

26 October 2026 | Culture | By WPS Staff

Sources: Consequence, Fox 4 News

The company behind the Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders has taken legal action against rock band My Chemical Romance over costumes worn by dancers at a concert in Los Angeles. Pro Silver Star Ltd, which owns the cheerleaders brand, asked a court for a temporary restraining order and an injunction to stop the band from using the look-alike uniforms in future shows. The company also sought unspecified monetary damages.

The dispute centres on the band's first of five sold-out Black Parade shows at the Hollywood Bowl on October 21. During the song Teenagers, dancers dressed in outfits resembling the cheerleaders' blue and white uniforms took the stage and performed a routine. They then picked up prop rifles and took part in a staged firefight, appearing to defend the band from militarized forces before being depicted as shot.

In its filing, the company said the performance degraded its brand and damaged its reputation. It said audience members and social media users understandably believed that the performers were the real Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders, and that those posts continued to circulate online. The company described its cheerleaders brand as family friendly and said the violent stage sequence put that image at risk.

The company said it was not asking the band to change its music, its message, its setlist, its choreography, or even its decision to put performers with prop rifles on stage. It said it asked only that those performers not wear the Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders uniform while doing it, adding that the range of alternative costume designs was effectively unlimited. According to the filing, the company had earlier sent a cease-and-desist letter, but the band declined to stop using the costumes.

Representatives for My Chemical Romance and concert promoter Live Nation had not publicly commented on the lawsuit at the time of reporting. The court was asked to block further use of the uniforms while the case proceeds, leaving the band's remaining Hollywood Bowl shows and the costume question at the centre of the dispute.
