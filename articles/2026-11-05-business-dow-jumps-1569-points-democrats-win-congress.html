Dow jumps 1,569 points after Democrats win Congress

Wall Street surged on Wednesday after Democrats won control of Congress, with the Dow jumping 1,569 points as oil prices fell sharply on hopes the Iran war could end.

5 November 2026 | Business | By WPS Staff

Sources: CNN, Nation Thailand

Wall Street surged on Wednesday, 4 November, after Democrats won control of Congress in the midterm elections. The Dow Jones Industrial Average jumped 1,569 points, or 3 percent, to close at 53,356, its best day since April 2025 and its biggest gain on the day after a midterm election since 1982. The S&P 500 rose 1.58 percent to 7,688, while the Nasdaq Composite gained 1.44 percent to 25,736.

Stock futures had fallen on Tuesday night as the results came in, but they recovered overnight and kept rising through Wednesday. Investors welcomed the certainty of a clear result and the prospect of a divided government, which many in the market read as a check on sharp policy changes and a more predictable outlook for spending and taxes.

Oil prices fell sharply at the same time. Brent crude dropped almost 8.7 percent to settle at 94.62 dollars a barrel, its lowest close since 31 August, after Vice President JD Vance said the war with Iran was the reason Republicans lost. Traders took the comments as a sign that pressure could grow for a negotiated end to the war, which would ease energy costs.

Bond yields also fell and the dollar weakened, while a measure of expected market volatility sank almost 20 percent. Large technology shares led the rally, with Microsoft up 6.7 percent and Amazon up 6.1 percent, as investors bet that a slower pace of data centre building could leave the biggest companies with more cash to spare.

Not every part of the market rose. Shares of data centre operators and suppliers fell sharply on worries about tighter rules on artificial intelligence building, and defence shares dropped as investors weighed stronger congressional opposition to a large defence budget request. Analysts also urged caution, noting that the new Congress will not take its seats until January and that President Donald Trump still holds veto power.
